Comparing NYC Observation Decks: Edge vs Top of the Rock vs Empire State Building

· ⏱️ 14 minute read

Last updated: September 30, 2026

New York’s observation decks offer very different experiences. Top of the Rock is best for photographing the Empire State Building, the Empire State Building itself combines history with open-air views, and Edge delivers the most dramatic outdoor experience. SUMMIT focuses on immersive art, while One World Observatory is ideal for Lower Manhattan and harbor views.

This guide compares the three classic NYC observation decks in detail and also explains how the newer alternatives fit into your itinerary, budget, and travel plans.

⚡ Quick answer: Which NYC observation deck is best?

Choose Top of the Rock for the best Empire State Building photograph, the Empire State Building for history and exhibits, and Edge for an outdoor thrill. Choose SUMMIT One Vanderbilt for immersive art and creative photographs, One World Observatory for harbor and Lower Manhattan views, Centre 360 for a free civic viewpoint, and the Times Square Skywalk for Times Square itself.

1. Edge at Hudson Yards

Best for: outdoor thrills and Hudson River sunsets

Location: 30 Hudson Yards, West Midtown Manhattan
Height: approximately 1,100 feet above Hudson Yards; official materials list 1,131 feet
Reserve now and Pay later →

Edge is one of New York’s most dramatic outdoor observation experiences. The attraction includes a glass floor, angled glass walls, outdoor viewing areas, skyline seating, and an eastern-facing point extending over the city. Every standard ticket also includes indoor immersive installations and indoor viewing areas.

My Experience: Stepping onto the Edge outdoor deck feels like floating above the city. The glass floor segment is thrilling, and the views toward the Hudson River and Midtown skyline are unbeatable, especially at sunset or night.

Best For

Outdoor-experience seekers, photographers, couples seeking sunset or night views over the Hudson River, visitors who want something modern and dramatic.

Tips

Standard tickets start around $42. Advance-saver tickets may start around $34 when purchased at least 14 days ahead. Sunset is especially attractive because of the Hudson River position. Weekdays are less crowded than weekends.

Edge NYC at 30 Hudson Yards — one of New York’s highest outdoor observation experiences. The glass floor and angled glass walls extend over the city; the ticket also includes indoor immersive areas. Take the No. 7 subway to 34th Street–Hudson Yards.

2. Top of the Rock

Related Post
  • Is Top of the Rock Worth It? Honest Review & Visitor Tips

Best for: the classic New York skyline photograph

Location: 30 Rockefeller Plaza, Midtown Manhattan (main entrance at 50 West 50th Street)
Height: approximately 850 feet above street level (open-air 70th-floor terrace)
Reserve now and Pay later →

Top of the Rock is the strongest choice for photographing the Empire State Building with Central Park and Midtown Manhattan behind it. The attraction occupies three observation levels of Rockefeller Center and ends with an open-air 70th-floor terrace. Top of the Rock has indoor viewing areas, but its signature upper terrace is outdoors. Wind, rain, snow, and poor visibility can affect the experience.

My Experience: I love Top of the Rock for the classic NYC postcard shots. For the most dramatic experience, book approximately 60–90 minutes before sunset so you can see the skyline in daylight, during golden hour, and after dark. Sunrise and early morning visits are often the quietest.

Best For

First-time visitors, photographers wanting Central Park and the Empire State Building in one frame, families, visitors who want the definitive Manhattan skyline view.

Tips

Standard admission from approximately $42. Timed tickets reduce waiting. Book 60–90 minutes before sunset for changing light. Optional Beam and SKYLIFT upgrades available.

The Manhattan skyline from Top of the Rock at Rockefeller Center — the view most visitors picture when they imagine New York City from above. The Empire State Building sits directly in the sightline, which is the defining advantage of this deck over all others.

3. Empire State Building

Related Post
  • Is the Empire State Building Worth It?

Best for: history, exhibits, and visiting an iconic landmark

Location: 350 5th Ave, Midtown Manhattan
Reserve now and Pay later →

The Empire State Building is more than an observation deck. It is a historic New York landmark with museum exhibits, architectural details, and two different viewing experiences. The 86th-floor Main Deck includes an open-air viewing area. The optional 102nd-floor observatory is enclosed with floor-to-ceiling windows at approximately 1,250 feet above street level. The building also features exhibits on the second and 80th floors.

For most visitors, the 86th floor is enough because it provides the open-air experience and the most recognizable views. The 102nd floor is worth adding if you want a fully enclosed viewpoint, floor-to-ceiling windows, or a higher perspective in clear weather.

My Experience: The open-air 86th floor is my favorite for nighttime views — Manhattan lights up like a grid of gold and white. The building’s exhibits downstairs add another layer to the visit.

Best For

Classic NYC experience, history lovers, nighttime photography, visitors who want an open-air deck with museum-quality exhibits.

Tips

Current Main Deck prices recently started around $46. Book timed tickets. The busiest periods are the first two hours after opening and the hour after sunset. Midday, around 12–2 p.m., can sometimes be quieter. Prices vary by date and ticket type.

The 86th-floor open-air observatory at the Empire State Building — the deck that has defined the NYC skyline experience for generations of visitors. The open-air setting creates a direct connection to the city that enclosed decks cannot match.

Other NYC observation decks worth considering

The three attractions above are the classic NYC observation-deck comparison. New York also has several newer or more specialized viewpoints. These are not all direct substitutes for Edge, Top of the Rock, or the Empire State Building, but they may be a better match depending on your interests and itinerary.

SUMMIT One Vanderbilt

Best for: immersive art and creative photography

SUMMIT One Vanderbilt is not a traditional observation deck. It is a three-level immersive art experience with skyline views, located near Grand Central Terminal. The attraction includes mirrored rooms, reflective floors, glass ledges, outdoor areas, and the optional Ascent glass elevator.

  • Located near Grand Central Terminal
  • Mirrors and reflective floors throughout
  • Levitation glass ledges
  • Outdoor terrace included
  • Optional Ascent glass elevator experience
  • Typical visit approximately 90 minutes
  • Pants, shorts, or tights recommended because of reflective floors
  • Sunglasses useful on bright days
  • General admission currently starts around $44

Choose SUMMIT for immersive art, mirrors, and creative photos — not if your only goal is a traditional unobstructed skyline panorama.

One World Observatory

Best for: harbor views, Lower Manhattan, and poor weather

One World Observatory occupies floors 100–102 of One World Trade Center. It is fully enclosed and provides strong views of the Statue of Liberty, New York Harbor, the Brooklyn Bridge, the Manhattan Bridge, and Lower Manhattan. It is the best choice on cold, windy, or rainy days.

  • Fully enclosed experience
  • Floors 100–102 of One World Trade Center
  • Approximately 1,250 feet above street level
  • Typical visit of 45–60 minutes
  • Standard admission currently starts around $39
  • Timed tickets; no re-entry
  • Strong choice for cold, windy, or rainy days
  • Enter at least one hour before sunset for sunset views

One World Observatory is the best choice if you want harbor and Lower Manhattan views or prefer a completely enclosed observation deck. Read our full One World Observatory review.

Centre 360

Best for: a free Lower Manhattan viewpoint

Centre 360 is a free observation experience inside the cupola of the David N. Dinkins Municipal Building at 1 Centre Street. Availability is weekday daytime only, and a reservation is required. Sessions are limited and sell out quickly, so early planning is essential.

  • Free admission
  • Weekday daytime availability only
  • Reservation required; reservations are limited and sell out quickly
  • Approximately 15-minute viewing period per session
  • Allow about one hour for the entire visit
  • Views of City Hall, the Brooklyn Bridge, Manhattan Bridge, Lower Manhattan, and the East River
  • Closed-toe shoes required
  • Sessions can accommodate up to eight visitors; verify current limits on the reservation page

Centre 360 is not a replacement for a major commercial observatory, but it is an unusual free viewpoint for visitors who can obtain a reservation.

Times Square Skywalk

Best for: Times Square after dark

The Times Square Skywalk is located at One Times Square, 1475 Broadway. It focuses on Times Square itself rather than providing a broad Manhattan skyline panorama. The attraction is best treated as a short Times Square experience rather than a substitute for a traditional observation deck.

  • Located at 1475 Broadway (One Times Square)
  • Glass walkway with wraparound Times Square views
  • Views of the New Year’s Eve Ball
  • Timed admission; no on-site box office — purchase online
  • Current tickets from approximately $30
  • Hours vary; best after dark for illuminated signs

This is best after dark and works well before or after a Broadway show.

  • Empire State Building (350 5th Ave): B/D/F/M/N/Q/R/W to 34th Street–Herald Square
  • SUMMIT One Vanderbilt: 4/5/6/7/S to Grand Central–42nd Street
  • One World Observatory: Multiple lines to Fulton Center or Cortlandt Street–WTC
  • Centre 360 (1 Centre Street): 4/5/6 to Brooklyn Bridge–City Hall, or R/W to City Hall
  • Times Square Skywalk (1475 Broadway): Multiple lines to Times Square–42nd Street

Booking and planning

  • Book timed tickets in advance for all major observatories, especially for weekend and sunset slots.
  • Sunset times vary throughout the year. Book approximately 60–90 minutes before sunset to see daylight, golden hour, and darkness in a single visit.
  • Weekdays are generally less crowded than weekends at every major observatory.
  • Centre 360 reservations open in limited batches; book as early as possible.
  • The Times Square Skywalk has no on-site box office; tickets must be purchased online before arrival.
  • One World Observatory has no re-entry; plan your visit time carefully.
  • SUMMIT One Vanderbilt visitors should wear pants, shorts, or tights because of the reflective floors.
  • Centre 360 requires closed-toe shoes.

Frequently asked questions

Which observation deck is highest?▾
One World Observatory and the Empire State Building’s 102nd-floor observatory are both approximately 1,250 feet above street level. Edge is the relevant choice if you specifically want an outdoor observation deck at a similar height.
Which deck has the best Empire State Building view?▾
Top of the Rock provides the clearest classic composition because the Empire State Building is one of the main subjects of the view from its open-air terrace.
Which observation deck is best in the rain?▾
One World Observatory is completely enclosed and the strongest choice in poor weather. SUMMIT and the Empire State Building’s 102nd floor are also reasonable options. Edge has indoor areas, but poor visibility reduces the appeal of its outdoor deck considerably.
Do I need to visit more than one observation deck?▾
Usually not. Visit two if skyline photography is a major priority, if you want both indoor and outdoor experiences, or if a city pass makes the second visit more economical.
How far ahead should I book?▾
Book sunset, weekend, and holiday time slots as early as possible. Centre 360 requires particularly early planning because free reservations are limited and sell out quickly.
What should I wear?▾
Wear comfortable shoes to all observation decks. At SUMMIT One Vanderbilt, pants, shorts, or tights are recommended because of the reflective floors. Centre 360 requires closed-toe shoes.
Are all observation decks included in city passes?▾
No. City passes include some major attractions, but current pass listings do not include every observatory. Check the exact pass contents before purchasing, as the included attractions vary by pass type.
Can you visit all three classic decks in one day?▾
Yes, but two in one day is more comfortable unless you start early. Time each visit with golden hour or night lights for the best experience at each deck.
Is sunset always the best time?▾
Sunset is stunning but crowded. Morning offers smaller crowds and soft light; night gives the full city light experience. Midday at the Empire State Building can sometimes mean shorter queues.
Which deck is best for kids and families?▾
Top of the Rock for a relaxed family experience; Empire State Building for classic history; Edge if children enjoy heights and glass floors.

Final recommendation

The classic NYC observation deck choice comes down to three options: Top of the Rock for the definitive Manhattan skyline photograph, the Empire State Building for history and an iconic landmark, and Edge for a dramatic outdoor experience unlike anything else in the city.

Top of the Rock is the strongest single-deck choice for most first-time visitors. The skyline composition — with the Empire State Building, Central Park, and Midtown in one frame — is the view people associate with New York. If you want an open-air landmark experience with exhibits and history, the Empire State Building is the classic choice. If you want something modern and dramatic, Edge delivers.

The newer alternatives serve different needs. SUMMIT One Vanderbilt is best for visitors drawn to immersive art. One World Observatory is the right choice for Lower Manhattan and harbor views, or on a poor-weather day. Centre 360 is an unusual bonus for visitors who can secure a free weekday reservation. The Times Square Skywalk works as a short complement to a Broadway evening, not as a substitute for a traditional observation deck.

If you only have time for one deck: choose Top of the Rock if the skyline photograph is your priority, or the Empire State Building if you want the landmark experience.
