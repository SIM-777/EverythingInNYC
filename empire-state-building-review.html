Is the Empire State Building Worth It? An Honest 2026 Visitor Guide

📅 Updated 2026 · ⏱️ 11 minute read

If you’re only going to pay for one observation deck in New York City, should it be the Empire State Building? After comparing every major observation deck and digging through hundreds of real visitor experiences, here’s the honest answer.

Quick Verdict

If you’re looking for a one-sentence answer, here it is: Yes, the Empire State Building is absolutely worth visiting — but probably not for the reason you think.

Most people assume they’re paying for “the best view in New York.” They’re not. They’re paying to experience one of the world’s most famous buildings from the inside. That’s an important distinction. If your only goal is getting the perfect skyline photo, there are observation decks with arguably better views. But if this is your first trip to New York and you’ve dreamed of visiting the Empire State Building since you were a kid, it’s still one of the city’s most memorable attractions. It’s an experience, not just an observation deck.

The Biggest Question Everyone Asks

Whenever someone is planning their first trip to New York, the same question comes up: “If I only have time and money for one observation deck, should I choose the Empire State Building?”

After reading hundreds of visitor reviews, a clear pattern emerges. People rarely regret visiting the Empire State Building. What they sometimes regret is visiting it instead of another observation deck without understanding what makes each one different. The better question isn’t “Is the Empire State Building good?” It’s “Is it the right observation deck for the kind of experience I want?”

Why the Empire State Building Is Still Worth Visiting

You’re Visiting the Building — Not Just the View

This is the biggest difference between the Empire State Building and almost every other observation deck in New York. The attraction begins long before you reach the elevator.

Walking through the restored Art Deco lobby feels like entering a living piece of New York history. The marble walls, gold ceiling murals, and iconic architecture immediately remind you that this isn’t just another skyscraper — it’s one of the city’s defining landmarks. As you make your way toward the elevators, you’ll pass museum-style exhibits covering the building’s remarkable construction, its place in popular culture, its starring role in films like King Kong, and the engineering achievements that made it one of the greatest architectural projects of its time.

Many visitors mention that these exhibits pleasantly surprised them. Instead of feeling like time spent waiting in line, they make the journey to the observation deck feel like part of the attraction itself. That’s something many newer observation decks simply can’t replicate. You’re not visiting a purpose-built tourist attraction. You’re visiting an actual piece of New York history.

Visibility makes or breaks the experience — a clear day looks spectacular, a foggy one is an entirely different visit.

The Open-Air Observation Deck Changes Everything

One feature consistently stands out in visitor reviews: the 86th Floor is outdoors. That may sound like a small detail until you’ve visited multiple observation decks. Several newer decks place visitors behind large glass panels — excellent for staying warm and protected from the wind, but not always great for photography.

Instead, on the 86th Floor you’re standing outside with nothing separating you from the city except the iconic safety fencing. You can hear Manhattan below. Feel the wind. Watch helicopters pass at eye level. Listen to distant sirens echoing through Midtown. It’s immersive in a way that’s difficult to explain until you’re standing there. Photographers often prefer the 86th Floor because it’s much easier to capture sharp skyline photos without reflections.

It Feels Like the New York You’ve Imagined for Years

Some attractions become famous because they’re genuinely spectacular. Others become famous because they’re historic. The Empire State Building manages to be both. Whether you’ve seen it in old Hollywood films, postcards, documentaries, or Christmas movies, you’ve probably been looking at this building your entire life. That emotional connection matters more than many people expect. It’s why visitors frequently describe the experience as nostalgic — even if they’ve never been to New York before.

Standing on the same observation deck that millions of visitors have experienced over the decades gives the visit a sense of continuity that newer attractions simply haven’t had time to develop. If this is your first visit to New York, there’s something satisfying about finally ticking off one of the city’s greatest icons.

But Here’s What Most Travel Guides Won’t Tell You

The Empire State Building isn’t perfect. And pretending otherwise doesn’t help anyone.

⚠️ You Can’t See the Empire State Building

This sounds obvious once someone points it out — but surprisingly few people think about it beforehand. One of the most recognizable features of the New York skyline is the Empire State Building itself. Once you’re standing on top of it, that iconic centerpiece disappears from every skyline photo you take. That’s one of the main reasons many repeat visitors eventually prefer Top of the Rock. From there, you get one of the most famous views in New York — the Empire State Building rising above Midtown with Central Park stretching out behind it. If photography is your number one priority, this is something to seriously consider.

📍 Sunset Is Incredible… and Everyone Else Knows It

Sunset is genuinely spectacular — but it’s also the busiest time of day. Expect longer security lines, more crowded elevators, and less space around the railings. If you’d rather enjoy the observation deck at a slower pace, many experienced visitors actually recommend arriving shortly after opening in the morning. The light is often clearer and the atmosphere is calmer. If you’re booking a sunset slot, plan to arrive 45–60 minutes before your reserved time — your ticket time relates to your admission window, and you still need to clear security before reaching the elevators.

86th Floor vs 102nd Floor: Is the Upgrade Worth It?

Many visitors automatically assume: higher equals better. That’s not always true. For most people, the standard 86th Floor ticket delivers everything they’re hoping to experience.

Many visitors who purchase the upgrade describe the 102nd Floor as a nice bonus rather than a must-see experience. The higher elevation is noticeable, but not transformative. For most first-time visitors, the outdoor 86th Floor delivers the iconic Empire State Building experience they’ve imagined for years. If your budget is limited, you’d probably get better value putting the price difference toward another New York attraction instead. That said, if you’re celebrating a special occasion, collecting observation decks, or simply want to experience every level of the building, the upgrade can still be worthwhile.

The Best Time to Visit the Empire State Building

If you ask ten people when you should visit, at least eight will say the same thing: “Go at sunset.” They’re not wrong — but they’re only telling half the story. The best time really depends on what kind of experience you’re looking for.

🌞 Fewest Crowds

Early Morning

The observation deck feels its calmest. Security lines shorter, elevators move faster, and you’ll have more space to enjoy the views without waiting at the railing. Morning light often produces clearer visibility.

  • First-time visitors
  • Families with young children
  • Photography (crisp, clear shots)
  • Visitors with limited time
  • Anyone who dislikes crowds
🌈 Most Dramatic

Sunset

You get three experiences in one visit: daylight Manhattan, the golden-hour glow, then the city transforming into millions of lights. That’s why sunset tickets sell first. But so do the crowds.

  • Couples
  • First-time visitors
  • Dramatic photography
  • Watching the city shift day to night
🌙 Hidden Gem

After Dark

Once the sun disappears, the deck becomes an entirely different experience. Illuminated windows, moving headlights, glowing bridges. Many repeat visitors say after dark is when New York feels most alive.

  • Repeat visitors
  • Couples
  • Visitors staying several nights
  • Anyone who loves city skylines at night
⚠️ Always Check the Weather & Visibility Before You Book

People spend weeks debating morning vs sunset but never check the forecast. Visibility matters far more than the time of day. A cloudy or foggy afternoon can dramatically reduce what you can see — sometimes the upper floors are partially hidden by low clouds, leaving visitors staring into white. On the other hand, a bright morning after overnight rain often offers some of the clearest views you’ll ever experience. Before booking non-refundable tickets, check both the weather forecast and the visibility forecast for that day. A clear Tuesday morning will almost always beat a cloudy Saturday sunset.

Is the Express Pass Worth the Extra Money?

The Express Pass allows you to bypass regular queues, including security and elevator lines. Whether that’s worth paying for depends entirely on when you’re visiting.

✅ Worth It If You’re Visiting...

  • During a holiday period
  • At sunset
  • On a Saturday or Sunday
  • With a tightly packed itinerary
  • For only one or two days in NYC
  • With someone who struggles with long queues

❌ Skip It If You’re Visiting...

  • Early on a weekday morning
  • During quieter travel months
  • With a flexible schedule
  • Happy to wait if it saves money
💡 My Advice

Don’t buy the Express Pass automatically. Buy it because your schedule actually needs it. Many visitors report moving through standard admission surprisingly quickly on quieter weekday mornings — when the building isn’t particularly busy, the Express Pass offers much less value.

The Mistakes First-Time Visitors Make

After reading hundreds of visitor experiences, the same regrets come up again and again. Fortunately, they’re all easy to avoid.

1

Assuming Higher Means Better

Many visitors upgrade to the 102nd Floor expecting an entirely different experience. Instead, they discover they’re mostly paying for a slightly higher enclosed viewing area. The iconic experience is still the outdoor 86th Floor. If you’re deciding between upgrading your ticket or putting that money toward another attraction, choose the second option almost every time.

2

Rushing Straight to the Elevators

The restored exhibits inside the building are surprisingly well done. Many people treat them as obstacles between themselves and the observation deck. Don’t. They explain how the building was constructed, showcase its role in popular culture, and add context that makes standing on top of it far more meaningful. Take twenty minutes and actually enjoy them — you’re visiting one of the world’s most famous skyscrapers, not just another viewing platform.

3

Only Thinking About the View

Most visitors spend weeks researching which observation deck has the best skyline. Very few ask which experience they’ll remember most. Those aren’t always the same thing. The Empire State Building isn’t just about looking at New York — it’s about experiencing one of the buildings that helped define it. That’s why so many people still choose it, even when another deck technically offers a better photograph.

4

Ignoring the Weather

Poor visibility ruins more visits than choosing the “wrong” observation deck ever will. If heavy fog is forecast, consider adjusting your plans if your ticket allows it. A clear Tuesday morning will almost always beat a cloudy Saturday sunset.

Can a Sightseeing Pass Save You Money?

For many visitors, yes. If the Empire State Building is one of several paid attractions on your itinerary, buying individual tickets usually isn’t the cheapest option. Several major sightseeing passes include admission to the Empire State Building alongside attractions such as the Statue of Liberty, Top of the Rock, museums, cruises, and guided tours. Depending on how many attractions you plan to visit, these passes can reduce your overall sightseeing costs by around 40–55% compared with purchasing separate tickets.

✅ The Key Rule

Don’t buy a sightseeing pass because it looks like a bargain. Buy one because you’re genuinely planning to use it. If you’re only visiting two or three attractions, individual tickets may actually work out cheaper.

Three Tips That Actually Improve Your Photos

Wear Dark Clothing at Night

Even though the 86th Floor is outdoors, you’ll encounter reflective surfaces in some indoor areas. Dark clothing helps reduce unwanted reflections when shooting through glass.

Secure Your Phone

The outdoor deck can be windy. Keep a firm grip on your phone whenever you’re leaning over the railing for a shot. A wrist strap is worth considering.

Put the Phone Down for a While

Many visitors leave with hundreds of nearly identical photos and very little memory of simply being there. Take the shots you want — then put the phone away and actually listen to the city below.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I spend at the Empire State Building?▾
Most visitors spend 1.5 to 2 hours from start to finish — including security, the exhibits, the elevator ride, and time on the observation deck. If you’re visiting during a busy period or at sunset, allow closer to 2.5 hours as queues can be longer.
Is the Empire State Building better during the day or at night?▾
Day visits offer clearer long-distance views and better photography conditions, especially in the morning. Night visits deliver the iconic NYC skyline fully illuminated. If your schedule allows, a sunset slot gives you the best of both worlds — though it’s also the busiest time of day.
Is the 102nd Floor upgrade worth it?▾
For most visitors, probably not. The 86th Floor delivers the classic Empire State Building experience — it’s outdoors, much larger, and offers incredible panoramic views. The 102nd Floor is higher and quieter, but the difference in view isn’t dramatic enough to justify the extra cost for most people. Save the money for another NYC attraction unless you’re celebrating a special occasion.
Is the Express Pass worth buying?▾
Only in certain situations. It’s worth considering if you’re visiting during holidays, on weekends, at sunset, during school vacations, or on a very tight schedule. If you’re visiting early on a weekday morning during a quieter season, standard admission is usually sufficient.
How far in advance should I book?▾
If you’re visiting around sunset, on weekends, or during major holidays, book as early as possible — popular sunset time slots often sell out well before your travel dates. Weekday morning visitors generally have much more flexibility.
Is the Empire State Building included in attraction passes?▾
Yes. It’s included in several popular New York attraction passes, including CityPASS and others. If you’re planning to visit several paid attractions, a pass can often save a significant amount compared to individual tickets. Read our CityPASS vs New York Pass comparison to see which option suits your itinerary.
Is the Empire State Building suitable for children?▾
Absolutely. Children generally love the elevator ride, interactive exhibits, and sweeping city views. The outdoor observation deck is enclosed with safety fencing, making it family-friendly. Morning visits are usually less crowded and more enjoyable for families.
Can I bring luggage or large backpacks?▾
Large luggage isn’t permitted on the observation decks, and there are no public storage lockers available. If you’re arriving straight from the airport or checking out of your hotel, store your luggage elsewhere before visiting. Small backpacks are generally fine — always check the latest bag policy before your visit.
Is the Empire State Building wheelchair accessible?▾
Yes. It’s fully accessible, including elevators and observation decks designed for visitors using wheelchairs or mobility devices. Check the official website before your visit for the latest accessibility information.
Is it worth visiting if I’ve already done another observation deck?▾
Maybe — but for different reasons. If you’ve already visited Top of the Rock, SUMMIT One Vanderbilt, or One World Observatory, don’t expect a completely different skyline. What makes the Empire State Building special isn’t simply the view — it’s the building itself. For many visitors, finally standing inside one of the world’s most famous skyscrapers is reason enough to visit.
