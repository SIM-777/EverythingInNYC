How to Get from JFK to Manhattan: Taxi, Uber, AirTrain, Subway, or LIRR?

Comparing every option for getting from JFK to Manhattan — matched to your hotel, arrival time, luggage, group size, and energy level

Arriving at JFK can feel like the trip has barely started and you are already solving a transportation puzzle. Which terminal are you at? Should you take the AirTrain to Jamaica or Howard Beach? Is your hotel closer to Penn Station or Grand Central? Will your luggage survive the subway?

The good news is that getting from JFK to Manhattan is very manageable once you match the route to your hotel, arrival time, luggage, and energy level.

My honest advice is simple:

  • Take the subway if you are traveling light and want the best value
  • Take the LIRR if you are heading toward Penn Station, Grand Central, Midtown, or Atlantic Terminal
  • Take a yellow taxi when you want the least decision-making
  • Use Uber or Lyft when door-to-door convenience matters, but check the pickup instructions carefully
  • Pre-book a private car for late-night arrivals, young children, or complicated luggage

My Favorite Option for Most Tourists: AirTrain Plus LIRR

RecommendedAirTrain to Jamaica, then Long Island Rail RoadBest for most visitors with luggage

For many visitors, the best balance is the AirTrain to Jamaica followed by the Long Island Rail Road. It is faster and more comfortable than the subway, especially when you have luggage, but it avoids the unpredictable road traffic that can turn a simple airport transfer into a frustrating ordeal.

At Jamaica, follow the signs for the LIRR. From there, trains run toward:

  • Penn Station — useful for Midtown West, Chelsea, Herald Square, Hudson Yards, and much of the west side
  • Grand Central Madison — useful for Midtown East, the Upper East Side, and hotels near Lexington Avenue
  • Atlantic Terminal — useful for Downtown Brooklyn when the schedule lines up
Important: You need a separate LIRR ticket. OMNY works for the subway and AirTrain, but it does not replace an LIRR ticket. Download the TrainTime app before leaving the airport, buy your ticket there, and check the destination and platform before boarding. Not every train at Jamaica is the right train for Manhattan, and not every train stops at Jamaica. Check the destination carefully.
MTA TrainTime app →
Why LIRR beats the subway for luggage: The LIRR has fewer stops, wider aisles, more comfortable seating, and generally less confusion. The main downside is the final walk — Grand Central Madison can involve a surprisingly long underground walk, and Penn Station is enormous. If your hotel is several blocks away, the last stretch may be harder than expected.

The Cheapest Option: AirTrain Plus Subway

Best ValueAirTrain to Jamaica or Howard Beach, then SubwayBest for light luggage and tight budgets

If you are traveling with a backpack, carry-on, or manageable rolling suitcase, the AirTrain plus subway is usually the best-value way to get from JFK to Manhattan. Most travelers take the AirTrain to Jamaica, then connect to the E train. The E train serves several useful Manhattan stations:

  • 42 St–Port Authority — for Times Square and the Theater District
  • 34 St–Penn Station — for Herald Square and Midtown
  • World Trade Center — for FiDi and Lower Manhattan. For what to do in Lower Manhattan: Lower Manhattan Walking Itinerary →
  • Spring Street, Canal Street, and West 4th Street — for SoHo, Greenwich Village, and nearby neighborhoods

You can also take the J or Z train from Jamaica for parts of Lower Manhattan, the Lower East Side, and Brooklyn. For hotels near the Financial District, the AirTrain to Howard Beach followed by the A train can also work well. However, Jamaica generally offers more route flexibility and clearer alternatives if there is a disruption.

The subway runs around the clock, which makes it useful for late arrivals. The tradeoff is that overnight service can involve longer waits, local trains, construction changes, and quieter stations.

OMNY tip: Use OMNY for contactless subway and AirTrain payment. Always use the same physical card or digital device throughout your subway journey so transfers and fare benefits are applied correctly.
OMNY contactless payment →
When I would not take the subway from JFK:
  • Multiple large suitcases
  • Hotel far from the nearest station
  • Traveling with a stroller and tired children
  • Arriving during severe weather
  • Unfamiliar with elevators, stair-heavy stations, or subway service changes
  • Arriving after midnight and wanting to reach the hotel simply

The subway is inexpensive, but it charges you in effort. For budget travel more broadly: How to Save Money on Your NYC Trip →

The Easiest Option: A Yellow Taxi

SimplestYellow Taxi from the Official Airport StandBest for low stress, large luggage, or tired travelers

A yellow taxi is still one of the simplest ways to get from JFK to Manhattan. Follow the signs to the official taxi queue outside your terminal. Do not accept a ride from anyone approaching you inside the terminal or near baggage claim. Licensed taxis use the official stand, have a visible medallion, and provide a receipt.

The Manhattan trip uses a regulated airport flat-rate structure, with additional surcharges, tolls, and tip. Because these charges can change, check the current fare page before traveling.

Traffic warning: A taxi can be wonderfully quick in the middle of the day and painfully slow during the evening commute. JFK construction has also created detours and changing road patterns, so allow extra time. A taxi is particularly attractive when two people are sharing the ride and each has a full-size suitcase.

For Brooklyn, Queens, or other destinations outside Manhattan, the standard Manhattan flat rate does not apply. The fare is metered instead.

Taxi vs. Uber or Lyft from JFK

Check FirstUber or Lyft from JFKGood for door-to-door convenience — but read the pickup instructions

Uber and Lyft can be excellent, but they are not automatically better than a taxi. The main advantage is convenience: you see the fare estimate in the app, can usually choose a larger vehicle, and do not need to explain the hotel address to the driver. The main disadvantages are dynamic pricing, pickup confusion, and construction-related rerouting.

Terminal 5 and 7 alert: Ride-app pickups from Terminals 5 and 7 may be directed to the Howard Beach AirTrain station because of ongoing construction. The AirTrain connection within the airport is free, but you still need to collect your luggage, find the correct platform, ride to Howard Beach, and locate the designated pickup area. Check this on the day you arrive.
Official JFK ride-share information →Current T5 and T7 pickup alert →

Before requesting a car:

  1. Follow the airport signs after baggage claim
  2. Open the app only when you know where the pickup point is
  3. Confirm the terminal or AirTrain station shown in the app
  4. Match the license plate, vehicle, and driver before getting in
  5. Ignore anyone offering an unofficial ride

Accessible ride-app vehicles are available, but if you need a ramp or wheelchair space, arrange it in advance whenever possible.

Is Uber Shuttle Worth Considering?

Niche UseUber Shuttle from JFKWorks only if your hotel is near a listed Manhattan stop

Uber Shuttle can be useful if your hotel is near one of its designated Manhattan stops. Current stops include areas around Port Authority, Times Square, Penn Station, Grand Central, Midtown East and Midtown West, Chelsea, Greenwich Village, and Union Square.

This is not a door-to-door airport shuttle. You reserve a seat, travel on a scheduled route, and walk from the Manhattan stop to your hotel.

Who should use it: A solo traveler with one suitcase and a hotel near a listed stop. Who should avoid it: Travelers with several bags, small children, a late arrival, or a hotel on the Upper West or Upper East Side. The service has luggage limits and schedule rules.

Private Car Services and Shared Airport Vans

Pre-BookPrivate Car or Shared Airport VanBest for families, late-night arrivals, and minimal decision-making

A pre-booked private car is the calmest choice for families, late-night arrivals, and travelers who do not want to make decisions after landing. The important part is booking ahead and receiving clear instructions for the meeting point. JFK construction can change curb access, so confirm whether the driver meets you inside the terminal, at a designated pickup zone, or at another airport location.

Shared airport vans are a reasonable middle ground. They can take you close to your Manhattan hotel, but they may stop at several hotels before yours. A good choice for a solo traveler with light luggage who cares more about a simple reservation than arriving quickly.

Use only authorized services: JFK lists authorized providers for van and shuttle service. Do not accept transportation from unlicensed solicitors at the terminal.

The Best Choice Depends on Your Arrival Time

Around 6 AM
The subway is already a reasonable option, and road traffic is usually kinder than later in the day. Choose the LIRR if a convenient train is departing soon, especially for Penn Station or Grand Central.
Around 9 AM
This can overlap with the morning commute. Trains are often more predictable than cars, but they may be crowded. If you have luggage, the LIRR is generally more comfortable than the subway.
Around Noon
This is the easiest time to choose based on your hotel. The subway is practical, LIRR is comfortable, and a taxi may be reasonably efficient.
Around 5 PM
This is when to avoid road transportation unless there is a very good reason. Take the subway or LIRR and let the traffic happen around you.
Around 9 PM
If the next suitable LIRR train is convenient, take it. If you are exhausted, traveling with children, or staying far from the station, a taxi or rideshare may be worth the extra cost and effort.
Around Midnight
For a first arrival after midnight, a yellow taxi or pre-booked private car is the usual recommendation. The subway is available, but late-night service changes and long waits can make the journey feel much harder than it looks on a map.

Traveling with Children

Families should think about luggage and car seats before thinking about price.

Car seats matter

A taxi is usually easiest for a family with tired children, but do not assume a car seat will be available. Bring your own or reserve a private vehicle that confirms the appropriate child restraint.

Strollers and stations

The subway and LIRR can work very well with older children. A stroller is manageable, but station elevators may be located far from the obvious entrance. Allow extra time and avoid making a tight connection.

For a family of four, compare the cost of one vehicle with four individual transit journeys. The decision is often less about saving money and more about whether everyone can comfortably manage the bags.

Weather Changes the Answer

Clear days

The AirTrain and subway are perfectly reasonable. Choose based on luggage, hotel location, and energy level.

Rain, snow, or extreme heat

The same route can involve wet sidewalks, slippery station entrances, crowded platforms, and a difficult walk from the final station to the hotel. Summer heat can be just as unpleasant as winter weather when you are carrying luggage through a large station. Do not underestimate the final walk.

During bad weather, prefer: LIRR for Midtown or Downtown Brooklyn • A taxi for door-to-door convenience • A private car for families or mobility concerns. For planning indoor options: Best Rainy-Day Attractions in NYC →

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1
Requesting Uber or Lyft before checking the airport pickup instructions — at some terminals, the pickup point may not be where you expect
2
Assuming OMNY covers the entire trip — it works for the subway and AirTrain, but the LIRR requires its own ticket
3
Boarding a train at Jamaica without checking the destination — not every train is going to Manhattan and not every train stops at Jamaica
4
Accepting an unofficial ride from someone approaching you in the terminal or near baggage claim
5
Underestimating the final walk — Grand Central Madison, Penn Station, and large subway hubs involve significant walking after arrival
6
Not checking JFK construction alerts before the day of travel — road patterns and pickup locations can change
7
Planning the subway with large suitcases when the hotel is far from the nearest accessible station

My Final Recommendation

Most tourists
AirTrain to Jamaica and then the LIRR. Comfortable, predictable, and much less stressful with luggage than the subway.
Times Square
The E train is hard to beat if you are traveling light.
Late nights, tired families, hotels far from stations
Yellow taxi or pre-booked private car. Consider the extra cost an investment in a smooth first evening.
