NYC From 10 AM to 8 PM From Penn Station: The Best One-Day Itinerary

A practical guide for travelers arriving at Penn Station or Moynihan Train Hall and leaving on an 8 PM train

The easiest way to ruin a short day in New York is to believe you have ten full sightseeing hours. You do not.

You have a city arrival, a station exit, perhaps luggage to deal with, subway decisions, crosswalks, queues, meals, bathroom stops, wrong turns, and a hard deadline at the end of the day. Once all of that is accounted for, you have roughly six useful hours—and that is still plenty of time to have a genuinely excellent day in Manhattan.

The secret is choosing one geographical story and sticking to it. Do Midtown properly, walk Chelsea and the High Line, explore Lower Manhattan, visit one museum, or cross the Brooklyn Bridge. You can have a memorable day. Try to combine all five and New York will reduce your itinerary to a series of escalators, turnstiles, and increasingly anxious glances at your phone.

This guide is built for the traveler arriving around 10 AM at Penn Station or Moynihan Train Hall and needing to be back for an 8 PM departure. It covers luggage, seven route options, transport, the return strategy, and what to skip.

The Short Answer

The best all-around plan for most first-time visitors is:

  • 10:00–10:45 AMExit the station, use the restroom, store luggage if necessary
  • 10:45 AM–1:00 PMBryant Park, New York Public Library, Grand Central Terminal, or the Empire State Building area
  • 1:00–2:00 PMLunch
  • 2:00–4:30 PMChoose one major attraction or neighborhood
  • 4:30–5:30 PMReturn gradually toward Penn Station
  • 5:30–6:30 PMEarly dinner near the station
  • 6:45 PMBe inside Moynihan or Penn, ready for the final boarding process
Key Rule

For most travelers, I recommend Midtown, Chelsea, or the High Line. They offer the best balance of iconic New York scenery, manageable travel, food, bathrooms, and a low risk of ending the day on the wrong side of town.

If your train leaves at 8 PM, treat 7 PM as your personal arrival deadline—not 7:45. Amtrak recommends arriving 30 minutes before departure and 60 minutes early if you need baggage or ticketing assistance. Gates are often posted only about 15 minutes before departure, so waiting near the correct concourse matters more than standing beside a giant departure board. amtrak.com

First: Understand Penn Station

"Penn Station" is not one neat building in the way many visitors imagine. The complex stretches beneath and around Seventh and Eighth Avenues. Moynihan Train Hall occupies the old Farley Post Office across Eighth Avenue, with its main entrance on West 31st Street. Amtrak uses Moynihan as its principal passenger hall, while NJ Transit and Long Island Rail Road use other parts of the larger station complex. Some tracks and concourses are shared. amtrak.com

The practical version is simple:

Amtrak

Moynihan Train Hall

Expect to spend your final waiting time in Moynihan, across Eighth Avenue on West 31st Street.

NJ Transit

Seventh Avenue Concourse

Use the NJ Transit concourse around Seventh Avenue—not Moynihan.

LIRR

Follow LIRR Signs

Follow Long Island Rail Road signs rather than assuming Moynihan is your destination.

Important

Confirm Your Railroad First

Do not leave based only on the words "Penn Station." Confirm your railroad and concourse before heading outside.

Moynihan is open roughly 5 AM to 1 AM. The LIRR concourse operates around the clock, although individual entrances and businesses have different hours. mta.info

Give Yourself a Station Buffer

A realistic first 45 minutes looks like this:

  • 10:00–10:10Leave the train and orient yourself
  • 10:10–10:20Find a restroom and refill water
  • 10:20–10:35Collect yourself, check the return train, decide on luggage
  • 10:35–10:45Exit toward the correct avenue

If you arrive early, wonderful—you have gained flexibility. If you arrive late, skip the station wandering. New York is waiting outside.

What to Do With Luggage

Luggage is the first major decision because it determines how freely you can move throughout the day.

Option 1

Amtrak Baggage Storage

Amtrak provides temporary baggage storage at Moynihan for Amtrak passengers and, subject to availability, members of the public with valid photo ID. Listed rates are approximately $20 per item for Amtrak customers and $30 for the general public (up to 24 hours). amtrak.com

The cleanest option if you are on Amtrak and want a station-based solution. Confirm closing time before leaving your bags—storage hours can matter.

Option 2

Pre-Booked Luggage Storage

Commercial luggage-storage networks near Penn Station can be cheaper for several hours. Some locations advertise from a few dollars per bag, but the real price depends on the partner, service fees, insurance terms, and opening hours. bounce.com

Use only if you have a confirmed reservation, a location open when you return, and enough time to collect bags before heading into the station.

Option 3

Keep Your Luggage With You

Reasonable for a small backpack or compact carry-on if you plan to stay close to Penn Station. A poor choice for the High Line, a full museum day, the Brooklyn Bridge, Lower Manhattan, or any itinerary involving multiple subway transfers.

Rolling luggage and New York sidewalks are not natural friends.

Common Mistake

A luggage-storage reservation is useful; a luggage-storage scavenger hunt at 6:45 PM is not. Book storage in advance, confirm opening hours include your return time, and always ask about closing time before leaving bags.

The Best All-Around Itinerary: Midtown Without the Panic

This is the itinerary I would recommend to most first-time visitors. It gives you classic New York, excellent photo opportunities, several food options, indoor escape routes, and a comfortable return to Penn Station.

Bryant Park — Midtown’s open-air living room with skyline views
1

10:00–10:45 AM — Arrive, Reset, and Leave Cleanly

Handle luggage, use the restroom, check your train status, and leave through the side of the station that best matches your first stop. If you are headed toward Bryant Park, Fifth Avenue, or Grand Central, aim for the Seventh Avenue side and walk east. If you are headed toward Chelsea or the High Line, use the Eighth Avenue side.

2

10:45 AM–12:15 PM — Bryant Park and the New York Public Library

Walk east toward Bryant Park. It is one of the most efficient "New York in one hour" stops in Manhattan: skyline, office towers, fountains, trees, chairs, seasonal installations, and the rear facade of the New York Public Library all arrive together. The park sits between Fifth and Sixth Avenues from 40th to 42nd Streets, and its public restroom is open daily from 8 AM to 10 PM—unusually valuable in a train-deadline itinerary. bryantpark.org

Step inside the Stephen A. Schwarzman Building if it is open and you want a quiet architectural break. Check current hours before treating it as a guaranteed stop. nypl.org

This is also the point where you decide whether you are an attraction person or a wandering person. For a major ticketed sight, continue toward the Empire State Building or Grand Central. For atmosphere, stay above ground and walk east.

3

12:15–1:15 PM — Grand Central Terminal

Grand Central is worth visiting even if you are not taking a train there. Walk through the Main Concourse, look up at the celestial ceiling, and explore the surrounding halls without turning the visit into a museum assignment. The terminal is generally open from about 5:15 AM until 2 AM, although individual restaurants and shops keep their own hours. grandcentralterminal.com

The route is simple: Bryant Park to Grand Central is a pleasant walk east along 42nd Street. If the weather is poor, use the subway or limit the outdoor section.

4

1:15–2:15 PM — Lunch

Choose food based on the next part of your day. Koreatown (just south on West 32nd Street) offers Korean and Asian food-court selection at Food Gallery 32. foodgallery32nyc.com

Eataly Flatiron at 200 Fifth Avenue is a polished market-style option, open from morning into the evening—a good choice if you intend to continue toward Madison Square Park or Chelsea. eataly.com

Los Tacos No. 1 has a convenient location at 201 West 33rd Street for a quick classic meal near the station. lostacos1.com

Do not spend an hour searching for the "perfect" New York lunch. Eat something excellent, reasonably fast, and geographically useful. For the best pizza recommendations near any of these areas, see our guide to the best NYC pizza for tourists.

5

2:15–4:30 PM — Choose One Major Finish

At this point, choose one:

Empire State Building

Best choice for one unmistakably iconic paid attraction. Reserve ahead, allow at least an hour, and remember that admission is time-dependent. Standard tickets commonly start around the mid-$40s before booking fees. esbnyc.com

Flatiron and Madison Square Park

Better if you want architecture, street life, and flexibility without committing to a timed ticket.

Museum of Modern Art

Works well in bad weather. Advance online purchase is sensible because same-day access can be limited. moma.org

Fifth Avenue and Rockefeller Center

No ticket required for the exterior. A flexible option that works well as a stroll between Bryant Park and a later stop.

6

4:30–5:30 PM — Begin the Return

This is where a good day separates itself from a stressful one. Start walking west. If you are already near Midtown, you can take a slow route through Times Square—but do not make Times Square your final destination. It is a spectacular place to pass through and a terrible place to discover that your train departs in 25 minutes.

7

5:30–6:30 PM — Dinner Near Penn

Stay within a few blocks of the station: Koreatown, Hudson Yards, Chelsea, or the restaurants around Seventh and Eighth Avenues. If you want an evening out near the Theater District, our guide to what to do after a Broadway show covers the best dinner spots within walking distance of Penn Station.

If you want absolute simplicity, eat before returning to the station concourse. Station food is useful, but "we will just eat at the station" often becomes a hunt for seating and a restaurant that still has a manageable line.

8

6:45–7:00 PM — Enter the Station

Be at the correct station complex by this point. If you are on Amtrak, proceed toward Moynihan. If you are using NJ Transit or LIRR, follow that operator's signs. Check the departure board, find the relevant waiting area, and stay close enough to respond when the track or gate appears.

Seven Route Options

The Midtown itinerary above works for most people. But New York is a city built for individual preferences. Here are six alternatives, each built around a different kind of day.

1
Chelsea and the High Line Best for first-time visitors who want a stylish, photogenic day
  • 10:00–10:45Station and luggage
  • 10:45–11:30Walk west toward Chelsea
  • 11:30 AM–1:30 PMChelsea Market and nearby streets
  • 1:30–3:30 PMHigh Line
  • 3:30–4:30 PMHudson Yards or Little Island
  • 4:30–5:30 PMReturn toward Penn
  • 5:30–6:30 PMEarly dinner
  • 6:45 PMStation

Chelsea Market at 75 Ninth Avenue generally operates from 7 AM to 10 PM, although individual vendors keep their own schedules. It is an ideal place to split up for food. chelseamarket.com

Chelsea Market — one of New York’s best indoor food destinations

The High Line has multiple entrances around 14th, 16th, and 23rd Streets. Check the current map before setting out, particularly if anyone needs an elevator or accessible entrance. thehighline.org

The High Line is not a quick shortcut. It is a linear park with bottlenecks, crowds, stairs, and frequent pauses for photographs. Walk at a relaxed pace and leave before you feel finished. The return trip is part of the itinerary.

The High Line — an elevated park with neighbourhood and river views above Tenth Avenue
Best for

First-time visitors, couples, travelers who want photos, people who prefer neighborhoods to museums, anyone who wants several food options in one place. Less suitable for large luggage or travelers with mobility limitations unless the route is planned around the available elevators.

Grand Central Terminal — a must-see even if you are not catching a train here
2
Lower Manhattan and the 9/11 Memorial For visitors who want history and emotional weight
  • 10:00–10:45Station and luggage
  • 10:45–11:15Subway to Lower Manhattan
  • 11:15 AM–12:00 PMOculus and surrounding area
  • 12:00–1:00 PM9/11 Memorial
  • 1:00–2:00 PMLunch
  • 2:00–3:30 PMWall Street, Trinity Church, or Battery Park
  • 3:30–4:15 PMReturn north
  • 4:15–5:30 PMFlexible Midtown buffer
  • 5:30–6:30 PMDinner near Penn
  • 6:45 PMStation

The 9/11 Memorial and Museum are not interchangeable. The outdoor memorial is easier to fit into a short day. The museum is a more substantial visit and should be treated as the principal attraction rather than something squeezed between four other stops. Check current hours and ticket arrangements before committing. 911memorial.org

The Oculus is visually impressive and conveniently connected to several subway lines. Battery Park is a worthwhile endpoint in good weather, but do not turn it into an attempt to fit the Statue of Liberty into the same afternoon.

Do Not Attempt

9/11 Museum, Statue of Liberty ferry, Brooklyn Bridge, Wall Street, and Midtown dinner—all in one short day. The Statue of Liberty ferry requires advance planning, security, ferry timing, and a large block of the afternoon. Choose the memorial or the harbor. Do not make the harbor responsible for rescuing an overstuffed itinerary. nps.gov

3
Central Park and One Museum For travelers who prefer one serious cultural attraction

The Metropolitan Museum of Art is a wonderful museum, but not a casual one-hour stop. It is large, absorbing, and located far enough from Penn Station that transportation becomes part of the calculation. Current regular hours are generally 10 AM to 5 PM on Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday, with later hours on Friday; it is closed Wednesdays. metmuseum.org

  • 10:00–10:45Station and luggage
  • 10:45–11:30Subway or taxi north
  • 11:30 AM–2:30 PMMuseum (choose two or three departments, not the whole thing)
  • 2:30–3:30 PMLunch
  • 3:30–4:30 PMCentral Park southern edge
  • 4:30–5:30 PMReturn to Penn Station area
  • 5:30–6:30 PMDinner near Penn

American Museum of Natural History is a better choice for families and travelers who prefer spectacle to fine art. Generally open daily from 10 AM to 5:30 PM. One practical caution: the 81st Street subway station is not accessible, so travelers who need step-free access should plan around the accessible 72nd Street station. amnh.org

Central Park without a museum is a good option for repeat visitors, exhausted travelers, families with children, or anyone who wants an unstructured afternoon. Take the subway to the southern edge, walk for 60–90 minutes, and return south before the afternoon gets late.

4
Brooklyn Bridge and DUMBO The most cinematic route—and the easiest to underestimate
  • 10:00–10:45Station and luggage
  • 10:45–11:15Subway to Lower Manhattan
  • 11:15 AM–12:30 PMWalk across Brooklyn Bridge
  • 12:30–2:00 PMDUMBO lunch and photographs
  • 2:00–3:30 PMBrooklyn Bridge Park
  • 3:30–4:30 PMReturn to Manhattan
  • 4:30–5:30 PMMidtown buffer
  • 5:30–6:30 PMDinner near Penn
  • 6:45 PMStation

The bridge is busy, exposed, and more physically demanding than its postcard image suggests. The pedestrian promenade is shared with cyclists, and the bridge carries heavy traffic below. nyc.gov

Once across, continue into DUMBO and Brooklyn Bridge Park rather than turning around immediately. Pier 2 offers restrooms, water fountains, lockers, picnic areas, and accessible facilities. brooklynbridgepark.org

Local Note

This route is excellent in clear weather and unpleasant in strong wind, heavy rain, or extreme heat. It also leaves less room for a delayed subway or a long lunch. Check the weather before committing.

Brooklyn Bridge — cinematic, memorable, and a full half-day in itself
Brooklyn Bridge Park — waterfront lawns and Manhattan skyline views in DUMBO
5
Food-First New York Some travelers are not trying to see the city. They are trying to eat it.
  • 10:00–10:45Station and luggage
  • 10:45–11:30Koreatown breakfast or early snack
  • 11:30 AM–1:00 PMMidtown wandering
  • 1:00–2:00 PMLunch in Koreatown, Chelsea Market, or Eataly
  • 2:00–4:00 PMFlatiron, Union Square, or Chelsea
  • 4:00–5:00 PMCoffee and dessert
  • 5:00–6:30 PMEarly dinner near Penn
  • 6:45 PMStation
Koreatown (K-Town)

Korean barbecue, noodles, dumplings, and quick meals on West 32nd Street. Convenient to Penn Station.

Chelsea Market

Variety for the whole group. Best if you continue to the High Line afterward.

Flatiron / Eataly

Polished restaurants and the famous Italian food hall at 200 Fifth Avenue.

Hudson Yards

A convenient final meal close to the station before your train.

Food-first itineraries are underrated because they remove the pressure to turn every block into an attraction. New York is often most memorable when the plan is simply "walk until hungry, then eat somewhere good." For the best pizza in the city, see our guide to the best NYC pizza for tourists.

6
Bad-Weather New York Rain does not ruin New York. A bad route does.

Use indoor anchors that sit close enough together to avoid repeated exposure:

Moynihan Train Hall — impressive modern architecture worth a slow look

Koreatown — indoor food options just south of Penn

Macy's Herald Square — massive indoor shopping if needed

New York Public Library — architectural gem, free to enter

Grand Central Terminal — entirely indoors, beautiful, and central

Museum of Modern Art — full day anchor for serious art lovers

Chelsea Market — indoor food hall, warm and varied

Eataly Flatiron — open morning to evening

The best bad-weather route: lunch in Koreatown → New York Public Library or Bryant Park → Grand Central Terminal → one museum or indoor attraction → early dinner near Penn.

Skip the Brooklyn Bridge and the High Line in serious rain. A light drizzle can be atmospheric. Wind-driven rain turns both into endurance events.

7
Minimal Walking New York rewards walking, but you do not have to prove anything.
  • 10:00–10:45Station and luggage (store immediately)
  • 10:45–11:15Taxi or subway to Grand Central
  • 11:15 AM–12:15 PMGrand Central at a relaxed pace
  • 12:15–1:15 PMSeated lunch
  • 1:30–3:30 PMMuseum or Empire State Building
  • 3:30–4:30 PMCafe break
  • 4:30–5:30 PMReturn to Penn
  • 5:30–6:30 PMDinner
  • 6:45 PMStation
Accessibility Note

For a low-walking day: store luggage immediately, use the subway or a taxi to move between neighborhoods, choose one major indoor attraction, build in seated breaks, and keep the final meal near Penn. The 7th Avenue and 33rd Street accessibility improvements make the Penn area easier to navigate, but the broader complex remains large. Check MTA current accessibility information before relying on a specific elevator or entrance. mta.info

Subway, Taxi, or Walking?

The subway is often the fastest option, but it is not always the easiest option with luggage or unfamiliar travelers. The standard subway and local bus fare is currently $3. Express buses cost more. Use the MTA's current trip planner and service alerts immediately before leaving the station. mta.info

Walk When
  • Destination is within 20–25 minutes
  • You have no luggage
  • Weather is comfortable
  • Moving east or west across Midtown
  • The walk itself is part of the experience
Take the Subway When
  • Moving between Midtown and Lower Manhattan
  • Traveling uptown to a museum
  • Crossing several avenues
  • You have a firm timetable
Take a Taxi or Rideshare When
  • Traveling with multiple bags
  • Several people splitting the fare
  • Running late
  • Weather is poor
  • Need a direct route to Penn Station
Local Tip

A taxi is not a failure of New York travel. It is a useful tool when the city has already given you enough walking. Pay with a card—NYC taxis accept contactless and chip payments.

The Return-to-Penn Strategy

The return journey deserves its own plan. It is not the final five minutes of the itinerary—it is one of the day's major movements.

If Your Train Leaves at 8 PM

  • By 6:00 PMWithin 20 minutes of Penn Station
  • 5:30–6:00 PMEating dinner near the station
  • 6:45–7:00 PMInside the station complex
  • By 7:15 PMAt the correct waiting area

You may not need all that time. That is the point. A delayed train, a crowded subway platform, a closed entrance, a forgotten bag, or a long security line can consume 30 minutes without asking permission.

Check the Operator, Not Just the Station

Amtrak — Amtrak departure information for your train

NJ Transit — DepartureVision for NJ Transit

Long Island Rail Road — MTA TrainTime

Subway — MTA service alerts for disruptions before heading back

Do not rely on an old screenshot of a departure board. Track numbers and boarding information can change.

The Running-Late Decision Tree

4:30 PM — Midtown
You are fine. Walk west or take one direct subway trip toward Penn. Skip any new attraction.
5:00 PM — Chelsea
You are fine. Leave the High Line and return directly. Dinner should be near Penn or on the way.
5:00 PM — Lower Manhattan
Take the subway north immediately. Do not add Battery Park, a ferry, or another viewpoint.
5:00 PM — Brooklyn
Return across the bridge only if you are already at the Manhattan end. Otherwise take the subway. Do not wait for the perfect sunset photograph.
6:15 PM — Luggage uncollected
Collect it now. Food comes second.
6:45 PM — Outside Penn
You have recovered the day. Enter the station, locate the correct concourse, and stop sightseeing.

Common Mistakes That Cost Travelers Their Trains

1

Treating Penn Station as a Single Building

It is a connected complex with different railroad operators and multiple entrances. Confirm your railroad and concourse before leaving.

2

Assuming All Luggage Storage Is Open Late

Confirm the closing time before leaving bags. Storage availability, baggage-desk hours, and Red Cap assistance are separate matters with different schedules.

3

Starting With a Faraway Attraction

Do not make the first activity the Statue of Liberty, a distant museum, or Brooklyn unless you are prepared to spend much of the day in transit.

4

Scheduling a Major Attraction at 10 AM

You may not even be out of the station by 10:30. Build the first hour around arrival friction, not a timed-entry ticket you cannot use if you are late.

5

Eating Only at Peak Hours

A 1 PM restaurant queue can quietly destroy an afternoon. Eat at 11:30 AM, 2 PM, or 5 PM when possible.

6

Leaving the Return Trip Until the End

The return trip is not the final five minutes of the itinerary. It is one of the day's major movements. Plan for it the same way you plan your morning stop.

7

Overestimating Times Square as a Route

Times Square is close to Penn, but crowds make it slow. Treat it as a pass-through sight, not a transportation shortcut when you are running late.

8

Trusting a "Quick" Museum Visit

The building may be close. The entry line, security, ticket desk, elevator, and first exhibit are not always quick. Add at least 30 minutes to any museum estimate.

9

Trying to See Everything

This is the biggest mistake. New York is not improved by being experienced at double speed. Choose one story and tell it well.

A Realistic Budget

Your spending depends heavily on luggage and ticketed attractions.

Lower-Cost Day
  • Subway: a few dollars each way
  • Bryant Park and Grand Central: free
  • Casual lunch: approximately $15–25
  • Dinner near Penn: approximately $20–35
  • Luggage storage: roughly $20 per item at Amtrak (eligible customers) or less through a commercial partner
Mid-Range Day
  • Subway or occasional taxi
  • One paid attraction
  • Sit-down lunch
  • Coffee and a snack
  • Dinner

Plan on approximately $100–175 per person, excluding shopping and long-distance rail tickets.

Premium Day
  • Taxi or rideshare between neighborhoods
  • Empire State Building or museum tickets
  • Full-service meals
  • Premium luggage arrangements
  • Cocktails or a rooftop stop

Plan on $200 or more per person. Ticket prices change by date, time, and package. Reserve only the attraction that truly matters to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I see the Statue of Liberty in this time window?

You can see it from Battery Park or take a ferry if you plan very carefully. A full Statue of Liberty visit is not a casual add-on to an 8 PM train day. The ferry, security, boarding, island time, and return journey consume a substantial block of the afternoon. Statue City Cruises is the authorized ferry provider, and the last departure often leaves far earlier than visitors expect. nps.gov

Is it better to store luggage at Penn Station or elsewhere?

Use Amtrak storage for convenience and certainty when available. Use a nearby commercial partner if the price and opening hours suit you. Do not choose a location several blocks away simply because it is a few dollars cheaper—time spent retrieving luggage across the city is not free.

Can I walk from Penn Station to Times Square?

Yes. It is close enough to walk, but the crowds make the route slower than the map suggests. It is a fine first or final stop, not a reason to abandon your return buffer. Treat Times Square as a pass-through, not a destination.

Is the Empire State Building worth it for a one-day visit?

Yes, if you want a single iconic observation deck and are willing to reserve a time. No, if you are already trying to fit a museum, Central Park, Chelsea, and Lower Manhattan into the same afternoon. Reserve in advance—same-day queues can be long and time-dependent tickets mean you cannot postpone if you are running late.

Is the High Line suitable for someone with limited mobility?

Parts of it are accessible, but the route includes crowds, changing entrance conditions, stairs, and elevator-dependent access. Check the current map and plan around accessible entrances before committing to this route. The minimal-walking route (Route 7) is a better starting point for travelers with mobility concerns.

Which is better: Grand Central or Moynihan Train Hall?

Grand Central is the more historic and atmospheric visit. Moynihan is the more practical station for Amtrak travelers and has impressive modern architecture. If your train departs from Moynihan, arrive there early enough to enjoy it without turning boarding into a sprint.

What is the safest time to return to Penn Station for an 8 PM train?

For an 8 PM train, return to the station area by 6:45 or 7 PM. Earlier is better if you have luggage, children, mobility concerns, or a ticketed train with a complicated boarding process. Amtrak recommends arriving at least 30 minutes before departure, and 60 minutes early if you need baggage or ticketing help.

Can I leave my luggage in a hotel?

Only if the hotel agrees and you are a guest or have arranged a paid service. Never assume a hotel will store bags for a non-guest. Some hotels offer this as a courtesy for recent guests, but policies vary and it is never guaranteed.

The Final Recommendation

If this is your first short stop in New York, choose one of these four:

The Reliable Classic
Penn Station → Bryant Park → New York Public Library → Grand Central → Koreatown → Penn Station
The Best-Looking Day
Penn Station → Chelsea Market → High Line → Hudson Yards → Dinner near Penn
The Most Memorable Walk
Penn Station → Lower Manhattan → Brooklyn Bridge → DUMBO → Brooklyn Bridge Park → Penn Station
The Least Stressful Day
Penn Station → Luggage storage → One museum or major attraction → Early meal → Penn Station by 6:45 PM

My strongest advice is also the least glamorous: stop sightseeing before you think you need to. The best version of this day ends with you seated comfortably near your train, fed, organized, and slightly annoyed that you did not have another day in New York. That is a much better ending than running through Moynihan with a slice of pizza in one hand and a suitcase in the other.
