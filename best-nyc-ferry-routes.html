Best NYC Ferry Routes for Tourists, Ranked by Views and Usefulness

Every NYC ferry route ranked for tourists — which to take, which to skip, and which gives you the best views for the money

NYC ferries are one of the best cheap experiences in New York, but not every ferry route deserves a spot in your itinerary.

Some routes feel like a mini sightseeing cruise for the price of a snack. Some are genuinely useful for getting between neighborhoods. Some are mostly commuter routes that sound exciting on a map but make very little sense if you only have three or four days in the city.

The trick is knowing which ferry to take, where to sit, when to go, and when to ignore the water completely and just take the subway like a sane person. This guide ranks the best NYC ferry routes for tourists by views, usefulness, ease, value, crowd risk, and whether I would actually recommend them to a first-time visitor.

NYC Ferry Basics Tourists Need to Know

Before ranking the routes properly, you need to understand one thing: NYC Ferry is not the subway on water. It is scenic, comfortable, and fun, but it does not run every two minutes. You need to check the schedule, arrive early, and give yourself breathing room.

NYC Ferry vs Staten Island Ferry

This confuses a lot of visitors. NYC Ferry is the paid citywide ferry network with routes like East River, Astoria, South Brooklyn, Rockaway, and St. George. Staten Island Ferry is a separate, free ferry operated by NYC DOT between Lower Manhattan and Staten Island. The Staten Island Ferry is the classic free harbor ride. NYC Ferry is better for hopping between waterfront neighborhoods.

1. East River Route: Best Overall NYC Ferry Route for Tourists

Rank 1 East River Route
Views 9/10 Usefulness 10/10

The East River route is the one I recommend most often because it actually solves a tourist problem: it gives you beautiful views and drops you near places you probably wanted to visit anyway. You get Manhattan skyline views, DUMBO, Williamsburg, Greenpoint, Long Island City, Midtown, and Wall Street/Pier 11. That is a very strong return for one ferry ride. This is the closest NYC Ferry gets to feeling like a budget sightseeing cruise.

Lower Manhattan from the East River — the view you get on the East River ferry route. Manhattan skyline, Financial District, and the bridges all visible from the water. This is why the East River route ranks first for tourists: it doubles as a sightseeing experience on a working transit fare.

ViewsManhattan skyline, Brooklyn waterfront, bridges
Cost$4.50 one-way

Who should skip itNobody who can take it

The Route Split Warning

The East River route has A and B service variations. This matters because not all ferries stop at every pier. If you are planning to get off at a specific stop, check which service you are boarding. Getting on the wrong variant and sailing past your stop is a very fixable but annoying mistake.

Best Uses for Tourists

As transportation

Wall Street/Pier 11 → DUMBO or Brooklyn Bridge Park is one of the cleanest point-to-point tourist moves in the city. Beats the subway for the experience. Connect with: One Day in Brooklyn →

As a scenic ride

Pier 11 → DUMBO → Williamsburg → Greenpoint or back is an excellent loop. Sit outside, check the bridges, watch the skyline shift.

Sunset ferry

The East River around golden hour is genuinely beautiful. Aim for a mid-evening departure and you will understand why this route ranks first. For more waterfront view options: Best Free Skyline Views in NYC →

Final Verdict: Take this route. It has the best mix of views, useful stops, and itinerary flexibility of any NYC ferry option.

2. Staten Island Ferry: Best Free Ferry Ride in NYC

Rank 2 Staten Island Ferry
Views 9/10 Free
The Staten Island Ferry — completely free, runs frequently, and gives you some of the best harbor views in the city without buying a sightseeing cruise ticket. Stand on the right side heading to Staten Island for the best Statue of Liberty views.
CategoryDetails
ViewsStatue of Liberty, Lower Manhattan, harbor
CostFree
TimeAbout 25 minutes each way
Best directionManhattan to Staten Island for Statue views
Best sideRight side going to Staten Island; left side returning
Crowd riskMedium
Who should take itBudget travelers, first-timers, families
Who should skip itVisitors who want to actually visit Liberty Island

What Most Tourists Get Wrong

The Staten Island Ferry does not take you to the Statue of Liberty. It passes at a distance. You can see the Statue, take photos, and enjoy the harbor, but you are not getting Liberty Island access. If you want pedestal tickets, crown tickets, the museum, or close-up Statue photos, book the official Statue of Liberty ferry instead. Staten Island Ferry vs Statue of Liberty Ferry: What's the Difference? → • How to Visit the Statue of Liberty: Complete Guide → • Statue of Liberty guided tour tickets →

Scam Warning Near Battery Park

Ignore anyone aggressively selling "Statue ferry" tickets near Battery Park or outside the terminal. The Staten Island Ferry is free. You do not need a ticket. If someone near the terminal tells you otherwise, keep walking.

Can You Immediately Reboard?

Usually, you must get off at Staten Island and board again for the return. Do not assume you can stay on the same boat.

Final Verdict: The best free ferry ride in NYC. Not the most flexible ferry experience, but for a first-time visitor on a budget, it absolutely belongs on the list.

3. South Brooklyn Route: Best for Brooklyn Exploring

Rank 3 South Brooklyn Route
Views 8/10 Usefulness 8/10

The South Brooklyn route is excellent if you are building a day around Brooklyn waterfront stops. It is not as obvious as East River, but it can be more useful than people realize. Depending on current routing and seasonal service, it can connect places like Wall Street/Pier 11, Brooklyn Bridge Park Pier 6, Red Hook, Governors Island, DUMBO, Corlears Hook, Sunset Park, and Bay Ridge.

CategoryDetails
ViewsLower Manhattan, Brooklyn waterfront, harbor
Best stopsBrooklyn Bridge Park Pier 6, Red Hook, DUMBO, Governors Island
Best timeLate morning to afternoon
Crowd riskMedium
Who should take itVisitors spending half a day in Brooklyn
Who should skip itFirst-timers trying to "see everything" quickly

Why It Works

Red Hook is awkward by subway. Governors Island is seasonal. Brooklyn Bridge Park has multiple sections. The ferry makes these places feel connected. This route is best when you are not rushing. Think waterfront wandering, lunch, parks, and views. For how to spend a full day in Brooklyn using this route: One Day in Brooklyn: DUMBO, Brooklyn Bridge & Beyond →

The catch

It is easy to overplan this route. Just because a ferry connects two places does not mean it is the fastest option. If your plan is Times Square → Red Hook → Central Park → dinner in the Village, please take a deep breath and delete something.

Ideal itinerary pairing

Wall Street/Pier 11 → Brooklyn Bridge Park Pier 6 → Red Hook lunch → Governors Island if seasonal → DUMBO sunset.

Final Verdict: South Brooklyn is not the best route for every tourist, but it is one of the best routes if you want your day to feel slower, local, and waterfront-heavy.

4. Astoria Route: Best Less-Touristy Scenic Ride

Rank 4 Astoria Route
Views 8/10 Usefulness 7/10

The Astoria route is underrated. It is not the route I would force into a first NYC trip, but it is great for repeat visitors or anyone staying in Queens or Midtown East. It connects places like Wall Street/Pier 11, Brooklyn Navy Yard, East 34th Street, Long Island City, Roosevelt Island, Astoria, and East 90th Street.

The East River from Long Island City/Gantry Plaza, looking toward the Queensboro Bridge and Midtown Manhattan skyline — the kind of view the Astoria ferry route gives you. Uncrowded, relaxed, and a very different angle on the city than the more tourist-heavy routes.

Astoria Ferry vs Roosevelt Island Tram

The Roosevelt Island Tram is more iconic. The Astoria Ferry is more relaxed. If you have never done either, take the tram one way and use the ferry as your return. That gives you two different views without turning the day into a transit science project.

Ideal itinerary pairing

Roosevelt Island Tram → walk Roosevelt Island → Astoria Ferry or LIC ferry → Gantry Plaza State Park → dinner in Queens or Midtown.

Final Verdict: Astoria is not the obvious tourist pick, and that is exactly why it is good. Save it for a second visit, a slower day, or a Queens-based itinerary.

5. Rockaway–Soundview Route: Best for a Beach Day, Not Quick Sightseeing

Rank 5 Rockaway–Soundview Route
Views 7/10 Usefulness 6/10

The Rockaway ferry sounds dreamy: a boat ride to the beach from Manhattan. And yes, on the right day, it is fantastic. But this is not a quick "let's squeeze in a ferry ride" route. Rockaway is a real outing. Treat it like a beach day.

CategoryDetails
ViewsHarbor, open water, beach arrival
Best useSummer beach day
Best timeMorning outbound, late afternoon return
Crowd riskHigh in summer
Who should take itBeach-day visitors
Who should skip itAnyone with tight sightseeing plans

The Catch

The regular Rockaway ferry is first-come, first-served. On busy summer weekends, you may not get the exact boat you want. There may also be seasonal reserve options — like Rockaway Reserve or Rockaway Rocket tickets — that cost more but help guarantee boarding for specific departures. If you are planning a beach day in peak summer, this can be worth it.

Worth the reserve fare if

Going on a hot summer weekend • You hate uncertainty • You have kids or a group • You care about a specific departure

Skip the reserve fare if

You are flexible • Going on a weekday • You do not mind waiting • You are only casually considering Rockaway

Final Verdict: Rockaway is worth it for a beach day. It is not worth it as a quick sightseeing ferry for a short NYC trip.

The St. George NYC Ferry route is tricky because tourists already have the free Staten Island Ferry. So when does the paid St. George route make sense? Mostly when the route itself fits your geography: Staten Island, Brooklyn waterfront stops, Battery Park City, Midtown West, or Wall Street depending on current service.

If your goal is simply "ride a ferry to Staten Island," take the free Staten Island Ferry. Paying for St. George only makes sense when the paid route is actually more convenient for your day.

Final Verdict: Useful for certain itineraries, but not a must-do tourist ferry.

Best Ferry Mini-Itineraries

1. Cheap Skyline Afternoon
Start at Wall Street/Pier 11. Take the East River Ferry to DUMBO. Walk Brooklyn Bridge Park. Get pizza or coffee nearby. Walk back over the Brooklyn Bridge if your legs still like you. For the full route: One Day in Brooklyn →
2. DUMBO + Williamsburg Ferry Day
Take the East River Ferry from Pier 11 to DUMBO. Explore Brooklyn Bridge Park. Continue by ferry to Williamsburg or Greenpoint. Eat, shop, wander, then return by subway or ferry.
3. Lower Manhattan + Staten Island Ferry
Visit the 9/11 Memorial, Wall Street, Battery Park, then take the free Staten Island Ferry round trip. Stand on the right side going out for Statue views, left side coming back. For the full Lower Manhattan route: Lower Manhattan Walking Itinerary →
4. Governors Island + Brooklyn Bridge Park
Take a ferry to Governors Island, explore for a few hours, then continue to Brooklyn Bridge Park or DUMBO if seasonal connections make sense.
5. Rockaway Beach Day
Take the ferry to Rockaway in the morning. Bring sunscreen, a light layer, and patience. Consider reserve tickets on peak summer weekends. Return before everyone else has the same idea.
6. Roosevelt Island + Astoria Ferry
Take the Roosevelt Island Tram, walk the island, then ferry toward Astoria, LIC, or Midtown. This is a great repeat-visitor day.
7. Sunset Ferry + Dinner
Ride the East River Ferry around golden hour, then plan dinner in DUMBO, Williamsburg, Greenpoint, Seaport, or Long Island City. For more sunset options on the water: Best NYC Sunset Cruises →

Common NYC Ferry Mistakes Tourists Make

OMNY does not work
NYC Ferry does not use OMNY. Use the app or ticket machine.
Activating tickets early
Tickets are time-sensitive after activation. Do not activate until you are boarding.
Not checking schedules
Ferries do not run like subways. A missed boat means waiting, not just one minute.
Ignoring route splits
East River A/B service can affect which stops are served. Check before boarding.
Assuming ferry is faster
Often the subway wins on speed. Use the ferry when the ride is the experience.
Not checking service alerts
Weather can affect ferries significantly. Check alerts →
Wrong side of the boat
You miss the best views. Plan which side based on your route direction.
Tight reservations after ferry
Full boats or delays can ruin timing. Never schedule a fixed reservation directly after a ferry.
Rockaway as a quick ride
Rockaway is a real beach-day commitment, not a 45-minute tourist loop.
Confusing ferry types
Staten Island Ferry (free, DOT) and NYC Ferry (paid, citywide) are completely different services.
Battery Park hawkers
The Staten Island Ferry is free. Anyone selling tickets for it near the terminal is a scam.

FAQ: NYC Ferry for Tourists

Is NYC Ferry worth it for tourists?▾
Yes, but only on the right routes. East River is absolutely worth it. South Brooklyn and Astoria are worth it if they match your itinerary. Rockaway is worth it for a beach day. Some routes are more commuter-focused and less relevant to sightseeing.
Which NYC Ferry route has the best views?▾
East River has the best mix of skyline, bridges, and useful stops. Staten Island Ferry has the best free Statue of Liberty view from the water.
Is the Staten Island Ferry really free?▾
Yes. Completely free. Do not buy a ticket for it. If someone near Battery Park tells you otherwise, keep walking in the opposite direction.
Can you see the Statue of Liberty from NYC Ferry?▾
The best ferry for Statue views is the Staten Island Ferry. NYC Ferry routes are better for skyline, bridges, and neighborhood-hopping. If you want to actually set foot on Liberty Island, book the Statue of Liberty ferry separately.
Is NYC Ferry better than the subway?▾
For views, yes. For speed, usually no. Use the ferry when the ride is part of the experience, not when you are late for dinner.
Can you use OMNY on NYC Ferry?▾
No. OMNY is for the subway and buses. Use the NYC Ferry app or ticket vending machines at the pier.
Do NYC ferries run at night?▾
Some routes run into the evening, but schedules vary by route and season. Always check the current schedule before planning an evening ferry ride.
Are ferries crowded?▾
They can be, especially during commute times, summer weekends, and Rockaway beach departures. Arrive a few minutes early if you want a good outdoor spot.
Which NYC Ferry route is best at sunset?▾
East River. Aim for DUMBO, Williamsburg, Greenpoint, LIC, or Pier 11 around golden hour. The Manhattan skyline in the late afternoon is the best free sightseeing the ferry offers.
Can I bring luggage on the ferry?▾
Small luggage is generally manageable, but ferries are not ideal if you are hauling giant suitcases during busy departure times. It is not prohibited, but it is inconvenient.
What is the difference between NYC Ferry and Staten Island Ferry?▾
NYC Ferry is the paid citywide ferry network ($4.50/ride) with routes covering Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Manhattan waterfront stops. Staten Island Ferry is a separate free service operated by NYC DOT between Whitehall Terminal in Lower Manhattan and St. George Terminal on Staten Island.
Is the Rockaway ferry worth it?▾
Yes for a beach day. No for a quick tourist ride. If Rockaway Beach is your destination for the day, the ferry is a genuinely fun way to get there. If you are thinking of "quickly doing the Rockaway ferry" between other sightseeing stops, skip it.

Final Verdict: Which NYC Ferry Route Should You Actually Take?

The bottom line

If you want the best all-around ferry ride in NYC, take the East River route. It has the strongest mix of views, useful stops, value, and itinerary flexibility.

If you want the best free ferry experience, take the Staten Island Ferry. If you want a slower Brooklyn day, take South Brooklyn. If you have already done the obvious stuff, take Astoria. If it is summer and you want the beach, take Rockaway — but respect the logistics.

And if you are staring at the ferry map wondering whether you need to understand every route: you do not. Pick the one that improves your day. The ferry should feel like a bonus, not homework.
