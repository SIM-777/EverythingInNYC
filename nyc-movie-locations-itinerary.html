Famous Movie Locations in NYC: The Complete Walking Itinerary

📅 Updated 2026 · ⏱️ 10 minute read · 🚶️ Two walks · 6.5–7.5 miles total

I’ve watched more people waste an entire afternoon chasing movie locations across New York than almost any other kind of sightseeing. It’s not that the spots are hard to find — it’s that most lists just throw fifty addresses at you in no particular order, and you end up crossing the same three blocks four times before lunch. So I actually mapped this one out. Two walks, each one a clean, single-direction loop, connected by exactly one subway ride. No wasted blocks.

Fair warning up front: this covers real ground. Between both walks you’re looking at somewhere in the range of 6.5 to 7.5 miles of actual walking — and that’s before you stop to take photos, poke around Washington Square Park, or wander Central Park a little longer than planned. I’ll give you the honest split-it-up option below too, because I don’t think you need to do this in one day to do it right.

The Two-Zone Structure — Read This First

Here’s the thing nobody tells you: NYC’s most famous movie locations don’t sit in one neat cluster. They fall into two genuinely separate zones — Greenwich Village / Tribeca / Financial District, and Midtown / Central Park — roughly two miles apart with nothing much movie-related in between. Try to walk both as one continuous route and you’ll either backtrack constantly or waste an hour walking through blocks with nothing to see.

My fix: walk each zone as its own tight, one-direction loop, and take a single subway ride to connect them.

Walk 1

Greenwich Village → Financial District

The denser of the two walks. Four separate filming locations in the West Village alone, within a few blocks of each other. Start at West 4th St subway station.

Walk 2

Herald Square → Central Park

Tighter and more forgiving — almost the whole thing runs straight up 5th Avenue. Start at 34th St-Herald Square station.

The Connector

Between the two walks: Wall St to Herald Square, about 15–20 minutes on the 4/5 express. One deliberate hop, not a zigzag.

Note on the Phoebe’s-to-Katz’s leg: That’s the one stretch of this walk that doesn’t feel like movie-hopping so much as just crossing town — it’s genuinely the longest single connector on either route. Good spot for a coffee break, or hop the subway one stop if you’re tired.
Local Tips
  • Ross’s apartment building (19–21 Grove St) sits on the same corner as the Friends building — you’ll see both without any extra walking.
  • Carrie’s stoop at 66 Perry St is roped off by the owner now. Take your photo from the sidewalk and move on — the residents have earned the right to be tired of tourists on their steps.
  • The Ghostbusters firehouse is a working FDNY station, not a museum. The crew is generally friendly about a quick photo, but they’re not there for you — don’t ask for a tour.
Weekday note: The NYSE trading floor view only makes sense on weekdays, 9:30 AM–4 PM — the whole Financial District feels noticeably quieter on weekends, which changes the vibe of this walk’s ending if that matters to you.

Local Tips
  • A short detour to 259A W 55th St gets you the real Soupman location from Seinfeld — a few minutes off-route between Rockefeller Center and Tiffany’s, worth it if you’re a big enough fan to care.
  • The FAO Schwarz piano from Big isn’t at the store’s original address anymore — the store relocated to Rockefeller Center, and the actual piano prop moved with it. Don’t go looking for it on the old 5th Avenue block.
  • Central Park Zoo is ticketed, not free — don’t assume you can just wander in the way you would the rest of the park.

One Day or Two? Pick Your Pace

I’ll be straight with you: doing both walks in one day is genuinely ambitious. Here’s how I’d frame the choice.

Option A — One Day

Both walks, one ambitious day

Roughly 6.5–7.5 miles of walking, likely more once you factor in photo stops and wandering. Doable if you’re a strong walker and this is the main thing you want to do that day — but don’t stack anything else on top of it.

Option B — Split It   Recommended

Each walk on a separate half-day

Walk 1 one afternoon, Walk 2 another — either on consecutive days or whenever you’ve got a free half-day. Each walk on its own is a very manageable 2–5 miles, leaves room to actually linger at the spots that matter to you, and doesn’t turn into an endurance test.

Visiting Etiquette — Read This Too

Almost every location on this list is either a private residence, a working business, or an active building — not a museum built for visitors. A few ground rules.

  • Photos from the sidewalk, not the stoop. Carrie’s building specifically has roped-off steps because of years of tourists sitting on them.
  • The Ghostbusters firehouse is an active fire station. A respectful photo is fine; expecting a tour or a chat isn’t.
  • St. Patrick’s Old Cathedral and St. Patrick’s Cathedral are both active churches. Keep your voice down, especially if a service is happening.
  • These are people’s homes and workplaces first, movie locations second. Act accordingly and everyone — including the next visitor — has a better time.

Movie Locations NYC: Frequently Asked Questions

Can you go inside the Friends apartment building?▾
No — it’s a real, private residential building. The exterior is what you’re seeing on-screen; the ground floor is now home to a restaurant, The Little Owl, but the apartments themselves are private homes.
Is the Ghostbusters firehouse still a real fire station?▾
Yes, it’s an active FDNY station (Hook & Ladder 8). You can photograph the exterior, and the crew is generally friendly, but it’s a working firehouse, not a tourist attraction.
How far is it between the Friends apartment and Sex and the City locations?▾
Very close — Carrie’s building at 66 Perry St and the Friends building at 90 Bedford St are about a 5–8 minute walk apart, both in the West Village.
Do I need a full day for this?▾
You can do both walks in one ambitious day (roughly 7 miles of walking), but splitting them across two easier half-days is the better experience if your schedule allows it. Each walk alone is a comfortable 2–5 miles.
Is the NYSE open to visitors?▾
The trading floor isn’t open to the general public, but the exterior on Wall Street is a classic photo stop, best seen on a weekday when the Financial District actually feels alive — it’s notably quiet on weekends.
Where’s the real FAO Schwarz piano from Big?▾
At the current FAO Schwarz location inside Rockefeller Center — the store relocated from its original 5th Avenue address, and the piano moved with it.
Are these filming locations free to visit?▾
Most of the exterior locations on both walks are completely free — you’re walking to buildings, parks, and streetscapes, not paying for entry. The Empire State Building, Central Park Zoo, and any food stops are the main costs. See our NYC trip budget guide for a full breakdown of what to expect at every spending level.
Is there a guided tour that covers these locations?▾
Yes — several operators run dedicated TV and movie location tours in NYC. A guided tour adds historical context and story behind each location that you can’t always piece together from a map. If you’re doing this solo, the self-guided route above works well; if the storytelling matters as much as the spots, a tour is worth considering.
