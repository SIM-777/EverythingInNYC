25 Best Things to Do Near Times Square (Within a 15-Minute Walk)

Local Midtown walking guide with ranked reviews, free routes, itineraries, food tips, and what to skip

Times Square is one of those places every New York visitor feels obligated to experience. And honestly, you should. The lights, giant screens, Broadway marquees, and nonstop energy are unlike anywhere else in the world.

But here is the mistake many visitors make: they spend an hour walking around Times Square, take a few photos, eat at an overpriced restaurant, and leave thinking Midtown is just a crowded tourist zone. It is not. Some of the best parts of Manhattan are hiding within a 15-minute walk.

A few blocks away from the chaos you will find one of the world's most beautiful train stations, a free historic library with incredible interiors, peaceful parks surrounded by skyscrapers, famous Art Deco landmarks, world-class museums, hidden gardens, and some of the best food neighborhoods in Midtown.

The secret to enjoying this area is not finding more attractions. It is knowing which ones deserve your limited NYC time. This guide covers the places worth visiting if you are staying near Times Square, including what is truly worth seeing, what you can skip, realistic walking times, how long to spend, best routes to avoid backtracking, where to eat instead of tourist traps, and free experiences that rival paid attractions.

Top 10: Detailed Reviews

01
Rockefeller Center The Most Complete Midtown NYC Experience
★★★★★ 10/10
10 min walk • Free to explore
Walk from Times Square10 minutes
CostFree to explore
Time needed30–60 minutes
Best forFirst-time visitors, families, photography
Crowd levelModerate (very high during Christmas)
The famous Rockefeller Center plaza during the holiday season — one of the most recognizable scenes in all of New York. Even outside Christmas, the Art Deco architecture and plazas make this the highest-value stop near Times Square.

If someone visiting New York asked me "Where should I go near Times Square if I only have one afternoon?" Rockefeller Center would probably be my answer. It has everything people imagine when they picture Manhattan: Art Deco skyscrapers, famous plazas, Radio City Music Hall, luxury shopping, street performers, seasonal decorations, and skyline views.

The mistake most visitors make is treating Rockefeller Center as a quick photo stop. They take a picture near the fountain and leave. Spend more time exploring. Look up at the buildings. Walk through the lower levels. Notice the sculptures and architectural details. The complex is much more impressive when you slow down.

What to See at Rockefeller Center

Rockefeller Plaza — The famous central area. Best known for the Christmas tree, ice skating rink, and summer events. Atlas Statue — One of the most photographed sculptures in Midtown. Great quick photo stop. Channel Gardens — A quieter section connecting Rockefeller Center with Fifth Avenue. Lower-Level Shops and Passages — Many visitors miss these. Useful during rain, winter, and hot summer days.

Best Time to Visit

Christmas is the most famous version, but crowds can be intense — go early morning for photos. Evening is one of the best times: the buildings glow and the area feels much more dramatic. Almost nobody should skip Rockefeller Center. The only people who might are repeat NYC visitors who have already explored Midtown extensively, or travelers who strongly dislike crowded tourist areas.

Nearby Stops

Combine: Rockefeller Center → St. Patrick's Cathedral → Fifth Avenue → MoMA.

Common Mistake: Only visiting the Christmas tree area. The architecture is the real attraction.

Verdict: One of the highest-value attractions near Times Square. You get the classic NYC feeling without spending anything.
02
Bryant Park The Best Free Attraction Near Times Square
★★★★★ 10/10
6 min walk • Free
Walk from Times Square6 minutes
CostFree
Time needed30–60 minutes
Best forEveryone
Crowd levelModerate

Bryant Park is proof that the best NYC experiences are not always the most expensive ones. After leaving the chaos of Times Square, this park feels almost impossible: a quiet green space surrounded by some of Manhattan's tallest buildings. It is one of my favorite places in Midtown because it works for almost every traveler. You can rest your feet, drink coffee, people-watch, take photos, use clean public restrooms, and enjoy seasonal events.

Unlike many city parks, Bryant Park feels designed around visitors. There are movable chairs, tables, gardens, fountains, food kiosks, and seasonal activities. Morning is best for quieter photos. Lunch brings great atmosphere, but is busiest. Evening is beautiful when the surrounding buildings light up. In winter, the Bryant Park Winter Village becomes one of Midtown's most popular holiday stops with a holiday market and ice skating. In summer, the lawn becomes one of the best free places to relax in Midtown.

03
New York Public Library (Stephen A. Schwarzman Building) The Free Hidden Gem Most Visitors Walk Past
★★★★★ 10/10
8 min walk • Free
Walk from Times Square8 minutes
CostFree
Time needed30–60 minutes
Best forArchitecture lovers, rainy days, photographers
Crowd levelLow–Moderate

The New York Public Library is one of the biggest missed opportunities near Times Square. Thousands of visitors walk past the famous lion statues every day, take a quick photo, and continue walking. That is a mistake. The real attraction is inside.

The Stephen A. Schwarzman Building feels less like a normal library and more like a historic museum. The marble staircases, grand halls, and famous reading rooms are some of the most impressive interiors you can visit in Manhattan without buying a ticket. This is exactly the type of place that makes Midtown surprising. You go in expecting a library. You leave wondering why it is not on every first-time visitor's itinerary.

What to See Inside

Rose Main Reading Room — The highlight. The enormous room features long wooden tables, green lamps, high ceilings, and historic details. It is one of the most beautiful public interiors in NYC. Astor Hall — The first impressive space visitors encounter. Look up at the architecture. The McGraw Rotunda — Another underrated interior with beautiful murals and details.

Morning is best for fewer crowds, better photos, and quieter atmosphere. This is also one of the best rainy-day stops near Times Square — you can easily spend 30–45 minutes exploring without getting soaked. Especially recommended for architecture fans, couples, solo travelers, and families with older children. Only skip if you have less than an hour in Midtown or strongly prefer outdoor attractions.

Local Tip: Do not just photograph the lions. Go inside. The interior is the reason this stop is worthwhile.

Common Mistake: Assuming you need to be a "book person" to enjoy it. The building itself is the attraction.

Verdict: One of the best free attractions in Midtown.
04
Grand Central Terminal The Most Beautiful Train Station in the World?
★★★★★ 10/10
12 min walk • Free
Walk from Times Square12 minutes
CostFree
Time needed30–45 minutes
Best forArchitecture, photography, first-time visitors
Crowd levelModerate–High
The famous main concourse at Grand Central Terminal — one of the great interior spaces of Manhattan and the perfect anchor for any Times Square walking route. The first time you enter, you will stop and look up. That reaction is universal.

Grand Central is technically a transportation hub. But calling it a train station does not do it justice. It is one of New York's greatest architectural experiences. The first time you enter the Main Concourse, the reaction is almost always the same: people stop walking, look up, and take photos. The ceiling alone is worth the visit.

What to See Inside

Main Concourse — The essential stop. Look for the celestial ceiling, iconic clock, enormous windows, and dramatic architecture. Whispering Gallery — A fun hidden feature: two people can stand at opposite corners and hear each other through the curved walls. Grand Central Market — A good place for snacks, coffee, and quick meals.

Morning is best for photography. Midday has more atmosphere. Avoid weekday rush hour if you dislike crowds. Everyone should visit, even travelers who usually skip architecture. Think of it as the anchor of a route, not the entire route.

05
Top of the Rock Observation Deck The Best Classic NYC Skyline View
★★★★★ 10/10
10 min walk • Paid admission
Walk from Times Square10 minutes
CostPaid admission
Time needed1–2 hours
Best forFirst-time visitors, couples, photographers
Crowd levelModerate–High at sunset
The view from Top of the Rock — the classic Manhattan panorama that most visitors imagine when they think of a New York City skyline view. The biggest advantage: the Empire State Building is directly in your sightline.

New York has several famous observation decks. For a first visit, I usually recommend Top of the Rock. Because the view tells the story of Manhattan. From here you can see the Empire State Building, Central Park, the downtown skyline, the Hudson River, and the East River. The biggest advantage: you can actually see the Empire State Building. That matters. A skyline photo without the Empire State Building feels less like the New York people imagine.

Sunset is the most popular option — you get daylight views, golden hour, and city lights, but also the biggest crowds. Morning is often underrated: fewer people, clearer photos, easier movement. Most visitors spend 45 minutes minimum, 90 minutes comfortably.

Best for first-time NYC visitors, couples, photographers, and families. Consider skipping if you already have another observation deck booked or your budget is very tight. Do not book every skyline attraction — choose one. For a classic NYC view, Top of the Rock wins.

Common Mistake: Arriving without a reservation during peak times. Popular sunset slots sell out.

Reserve Top of the Rock in Advance

Sunset slots fill up significantly in advance, especially on weekends. Book Top of the Rock tickets with reserve-now, pay-later flexibility on Viator to lock in your preferred time slot without full upfront payment.

For a complete comparison of every NYC observation deck, read: NYC Observation Decks Compared: Edge vs Top of the Rock vs Empire State

Verdict: The observation deck to choose for first-time visitors.
06
St. Patrick's Cathedral The Peaceful Surprise Across From Rockefeller Center
★★★★★ 9/10
12 min walk • Free
Walk from Times Square12 minutes
CostFree
Time needed15–30 minutes
Best forArchitecture, photography, quiet moments
Crowd levelModerate

St. Patrick's Cathedral is one of those places that looks interesting from outside but becomes much more impressive once you enter. The contrast is what makes it special. Outside: Fifth Avenue luxury stores, skyscrapers, and traffic. Inside: silence, stained glass, soaring ceilings, and historic details. It is one of the easiest ways to experience a completely different side of Manhattan within minutes of Times Square.

Look for the massive interior columns, stained glass windows, side chapels, and detailed altar area. Morning is best — the cathedral feels calmer before the Fifth Avenue crowds arrive. Great for architecture lovers, couples, photographers, and travelers needing a quiet break. Pair it with Rockefeller Center directly across the street. Do not make a separate trip.

Common Mistake: Only taking a photo outside. The interior is the highlight.

Verdict: A free attraction that feels much more special than most visitors expect.
07
SUMMIT One Vanderbilt The Most Unique Observation Experience Near Times Square
★★★★★ 9/10
15 min walk • Paid admission
Walk from Times Square15 minutes
CostPaid admission
Time needed1.5–2 hours
Best forPhotography, couples, unique experiences
Crowd levelModerate–High

If Top of the Rock is the classic New York skyline experience, SUMMIT is the modern version. This is not just an observation deck. It is part art installation, part immersive experience, and part viewpoint. The experience combines mirrored rooms, reflective surfaces, glass platforms, interactive installations, and skyline views. Top of the Rock makes you appreciate Manhattan. SUMMIT makes you feel like you are inside an artwork.

The biggest reason people love SUMMIT is the photos. The mirrored spaces create perspectives you cannot get anywhere else in NYC: mirrored infinity rooms, reflections of skyscrapers, and looking down through glass sections. Sunset is the obvious choice but expect the biggest crowds. Evening is a surprisingly good option as the city lights create a completely different atmosphere. Best for couples, photographers, teenagers, families, and visitors who want something beyond traditional sightseeing.

Consider skipping if you only want a simple skyline view, dislike interactive art experiences, or have already booked another observation deck and have limited time. Many visitors underestimate how long the experience takes because they expect "go up, take photos, leave." If photography matters more than atmosphere, a morning visit can actually be better.

Verdict: The best modern attraction within walking distance of Times Square.
08
Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) The Best Rainy-Day Attraction Near Times Square
★★★★★ 9/10
15 min walk • Paid admission
Walk from Times Square15 minutes
CostPaid admission
Time needed1.5–3 hours
Best forArt lovers, rainy days, culture seekers
Crowd levelModerate
Midtown Manhattan has several world-class art museums within 15 minutes of Times Square. MoMA is the best choice for a rainy-day visit or when you want a slower, culture-focused afternoon rather than rushing between landmarks.

Many visitors say "I'm not really a museum person." MoMA is the museum that often changes their mind. The reason is simple: you do not need to understand modern art to appreciate seeing some of the world's most famous works in person. Highlights include Van Gogh's The Starry Night, Picasso works, Monet paintings, Warhol pieces, and Dali.

Do not try to see everything — that is the fastest way to get exhausted. A better strategy: focus on famous works, visit the sculpture garden, spend time on the floors that interest you, and leave before museum fatigue begins. Weekday mornings are best. Avoid arriving after a full day of walking when you are already tired. MoMA is one of the best backup plans when New York weather changes suddenly.

Common Mistake: Trying to "complete" the museum. You do not need to.

Verdict: The best paid indoor attraction near Times Square.
09
Chrysler Building The Best Free Architecture Stop in Midtown
★★★★★ 9/10
15 min walk • Free exterior
Walk from Times Square15 minutes
CostFree exterior
Time needed10–20 minutes
Best forArchitecture lovers, photographers
Crowd levelLow

The Chrysler Building is one of the easiest attractions to underestimate. Many visitors know the Empire State Building. Fewer realize the Chrysler Building is arguably the more beautiful skyscraper. Its Art Deco details are incredible: stainless steel crown, geometric patterns, eagle ornaments, and dramatic lighting. The best part: you do not need a ticket.

Best Photo Spots: 42nd Street for the classic angle; Lexington Avenue for a better view of the full building. Blue hour is the best time — the building lighting creates some of the best free skyline photography in Midtown. Perfect for photographers, architecture fans, and travelers doing the Grand Central route.

Local Tip: Look up. The best details are above street level.

Common Mistake: Taking one photo and leaving. Walk around the block. The angles change dramatically.

Verdict: Possibly the best free skyscraper experience near Times Square.
10
Fifth Avenue (Midtown Section) The Most Iconic NYC Walk
★★★★★ 9/10
10 min walk • Free
Walk from Times Square10 minutes
CostFree
Time needed30–90 minutes
Best forFirst-time visitors, shopping, photography
Crowd levelHigh

Fifth Avenue is famous worldwide, but many visitors misunderstand it. The attraction is not shopping. The attraction is walking through one of the most recognizable streets in Manhattan. The Midtown section gives you Rockefeller Center, St. Patrick's Cathedral, luxury storefronts, historic buildings, and incredible street scenes. Best stops include Saks Fifth Avenue (especially during the holidays), the Atlas Statue near Rockefeller Center, the Tiffany & Co. flagship, and the Cartier Mansion. Think of Fifth Avenue as a walking attraction, not a shopping destination. The Midtown section is enough.

Common Mistake: Trying to walk the entire avenue. You do not need to.

Verdict: The easiest way to experience classic Manhattan in under an hour.

Radio City Music Hall The Most Famous Theater Exterior Near Times Square
★★★★ 4/5
10 min walk • Exterior free
Walk from Times Square10 minutes
CostExterior free
Time needed10–30 minutes
Best forTheater fans, architecture lovers

Radio City Music Hall is one of New York's most recognizable entertainment landmarks. Opened in 1932, it represents the golden age of Art Deco theater design. For most visitors, the exterior is the main attraction: famous marquee, neon lights, and the historic entrance. The interior tour is worth it for architecture fans, theater lovers, and history enthusiasts. For casual visitors, the outside is usually enough. Evening is best — the lights make it much more impressive. Works best as part of the Rockefeller route: Times Square → Radio City → Rockefeller Center → St. Patrick's Cathedral.

Verdict: Worth seeing, but not worth rearranging your entire day.

Broadway Theater District The Best Evening Experience Near Times Square
★★★★ 4/5
0–10 min walk • Free to explore
Walk from Times Square0–10 minutes
CostFree to explore (tickets required for shows)
Time needed15 minutes–3 hours
Best forTheater lovers, couples, families

Many visitors think Broadway means buying a ticket to a show. It does not. The Theater District itself is part of the New York experience. Walking around at night, seeing the glowing marquees, crowds arriving for performances, and the energy around the theaters feels uniquely New York. Look for historic theater facades, illuminated marquees, and performers outside shows. The area is especially energetic between 6 PM and 8 PM. Combine: Times Square → Broadway theaters → Hell's Kitchen dinner. If you want Broadway tickets, avoid unofficial street sellers and use official ticket sources.

Koreatown (K-Town) The Best Food Experience Near Times Square
★★★★★ 9/10
10–12 min walk • Varies
Walk from Times Square10–12 minutes
CostVaries
Time needed1–2 hours
Best forFood lovers, groups, nightlife

If there is one piece of food advice I would give visitors staying near Times Square: do not eat directly in Times Square. Walk 10 minutes south to Koreatown. This tiny neighborhood packs an incredible amount into a few blocks: Korean barbecue, noodle restaurants, bakeries, dessert cafes, karaoke bars, and late-night dining. It feels completely different from the tourist-heavy streets around Times Square. Korean BBQ is great for groups, celebrations, and memorable dinners. Many restaurants stay open late — one of the biggest advantages of dining here. Evening is best — the neighborhood has much more energy after dark. This is where I would send someone staying near Times Square for dinner. Better food, more interesting atmosphere.

Verdict: The best nearby neighborhood upgrade from Times Square dining.

★★★★ 4/5
10–15 min walk • Varies
Walk from Times Square10–15 minutes
CostVaries
Time needed1–3 hours
Best forRestaurants, nightlife, local atmosphere

Hell's Kitchen is one of the best examples of how quickly NYC changes. Walk west from Times Square and within minutes you move from giant screens, souvenir shops, and tourist crowds to neighborhood restaurants, bars, apartment buildings, and local streets. The main attraction here is not one landmark. It is the feeling of experiencing a real Manhattan neighborhood. The restaurants around Ninth Avenue are the main draw. Popular with locals, theater crowds, and visitors after Broadway shows. If you are seeing a Broadway show, eat here before or after instead of around Times Square. Do not ignore the west side of Midtown — many visitors never explore beyond Broadway.

19
Garment District A Quick Look at New York's Fashion History
★★★★ 4/5
5–10 min walk • Free

Most visitors walk through this area without realizing they are passing through one of New York's historically important neighborhoods. The Garment District was once the center of American fashion manufacturing. Today it is a mix of fashion businesses, office buildings, public art, and interesting street scenes. It is not a destination where you spend half a day, but it adds character to your Midtown walk. Look for the Garment District public art, fashion-related sculptures, old signage, and historic buildings. Best on weekday daytime when businesses are open. Good for photographers, repeat NYC visitors, and people who enjoy neighborhoods rather than only landmarks. Combine: Times Square → Garment District → Bryant Park.

Verdict: Not a must-see, but a nice layer of authentic Midtown.

Urban Hawker One of Midtown's Most Interesting Food Stops
★★★★ 4/5
12 min walk • Varies

One of the biggest problems near Times Square is finding food that feels memorable. Urban Hawker solves that problem. Extended Route Attractions (21–25)

The following five attractions are slightly outside the core 15-minute zone or require more planning, but appear frequently in Times Square visitor itineraries for good reason.

21. Diamond District

5 min walk • Free • 3/5

Located just steps from Times Square, the Diamond District gives visitors a glimpse into a very specific New York world: jewelry businesses, traders, historic storefronts, and intense street activity. Best for street photographers and repeat NYC visitors who enjoy unusual neighborhoods. If you only have a few hours, prioritize Bryant Park, NYPL, Grand Central, and Rockefeller Center first.

22. Hudson Yards (Extended Walk)

15–20 min walk • Free to explore • 4/5

Hudson Yards shows a completely different side of Manhattan — modern architecture, luxury retail, public spaces, and skyline views instead of historic skyscrapers. Main highlights: The Vessel (famous honeycomb structure), Edge Observation Deck, and the public plaza. Best pairing: Times Square → Hell's Kitchen → Hudson Yards → High Line. Worth extending your walk if you have half a day.

23. The High Line (Extended Route)

Usually beyond 15 min walk • Free • 5/5 standalone / 4/5 for TS route

Strictly speaking, the High Line is not a true 15-minute Times Square attraction. However, it combines perfectly with Hudson Yards, Chelsea, and Hell's Kitchen. One of the best examples of New York transforming old infrastructure into a public space. Best visited in the morning when it is less crowded. Save it for a half-day west side itinerary rather than forcing it into a short Times Square visit.

24. United Nations Headquarters

20+ min walk • Exterior free • 4/5

The UN represents a completely different version of New York: international diplomacy, East River views, and modernist architecture. Great for history lovers, families with older children, and travelers who have already seen Midtown highlights. First-time visitors with limited time should prioritize Grand Central, Rockefeller, Bryant Park, and NYPL first.

25
TKTS Times Square Red Steps The Best 10-Minute Times Square Experience
★★★★ 4/5
1 min walk • Free
Walk from Times Square1 minute
CostFree
Time needed5–15 minutes
Best forPhotos, people watching, quick stop

The TKTS Red Steps prove that sometimes the simplest experiences work best. This staircase gives you an elevated Times Square view, a better photo angle, excellent people watching, and Broadway atmosphere. Best at night for the classic Times Square lights, or morning for fewer crowds. Everyone already in Times Square should visit — it requires almost no effort. This is where to take your Times Square photo. Do not spend an hour searching for the perfect angle. Avoid buying Broadway tickets from unofficial sellers nearby.

Verdict: The perfect quick stop before leaving Times Square.

Where to Eat Near Times Square (Avoiding Tourist Traps)

The biggest food mistake: eating directly in Times Square. You are paying for location, not quality.

Koreatown

Best for: dinner, groups, late-night food. Korean BBQ, dessert cafes, karaoke bars. Walk 10–12 minutes south of Times Square. This is where I would send someone for dinner.

Hell's Kitchen

Best for: local restaurants, Broadway dinners, neighborhood atmosphere. Walk west from Times Square. Restaurants around Ninth Avenue are the main draw.

Bryant Park Area

Best for: coffee, quick breaks, snacks. The easiest food stop without leaving your route.

Grand Central

Best for: convenience, rainy days, families. The Grand Central Market has everything from quick snacks to sit-down dining.

Before Broadway: Choose Hell's Kitchen. Better restaurants, less touristy. After Broadway: Choose Koreatown. Late-night options. During Sightseeing: Choose Bryant Park area for an easy stop without leaving your route.

Things I Would Skip Near Times Square

Good travel advice is not just about what to do. It is also about what not to waste time on.

1. Spending Too Long In Times Square — See it. Photograph it. Move on. Most visitors are satisfied after 20–30 minutes.

2. Eating At Random Times Square Restaurants — Common problems: expensive, crowded, forgettable food. Walk 10 minutes. The difference is huge.

3. Visiting Multiple Observation Decks — You do not need Top of the Rock, SUMMIT, Edge, and Empire State Building. Choose one.

4. Walking Without A Plan — Midtown looks close on a map, but crossing streets, crowds, and traffic slow you down. A route matters.

5. Ignoring Free Attractions — Many visitors spend hundreds of dollars while skipping NYPL, Bryant Park, Grand Central, and Rockefeller Center. Some of the best Midtown experiences cost nothing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best things to do near Times Square?

The best things to do near Times Square are Rockefeller Center, Bryant Park, Grand Central Terminal, the New York Public Library, Top of the Rock, St. Patrick's Cathedral, and SUMMIT One Vanderbilt. For most first-time visitors, I recommend walking away from Times Square rather than spending your entire visit there. A simple route covering Bryant Park, NYPL, Grand Central, Chrysler Building, and Rockefeller Center gives you some of the best Midtown experiences within a few hours.

How far is Rockefeller Center from Times Square?

Rockefeller Center is about a 10-minute walk from Times Square. The walk is easy and takes you through some of Midtown's most recognizable streets. The best route: Times Square → Radio City Music Hall → Rockefeller Center → St. Patrick's Cathedral → Fifth Avenue. This is one of the best short walks for first-time NYC visitors.

Is Times Square worth visiting?

Yes, Times Square is worth visiting once, especially for first-time visitors. The mistake is spending too much time there. Most people only need around 20–30 minutes to experience the giant screens, Broadway atmosphere, street energy, and classic photos. After that, the best parts of Midtown are found nearby.

What are the best free things to do near Times Square?

The best free attractions near Times Square are Bryant Park, Grand Central Terminal, New York Public Library, Rockefeller Center, St. Patrick's Cathedral, the Fifth Avenue walking route, the Chrysler Building exterior, and the TKTS Red Steps. These attractions can easily fill a half-day without spending money.

Can you walk from Times Square to Rockefeller Center?

Yes. The walk takes approximately 10 minutes and is one of the easiest and most enjoyable walks in Midtown. A recommended route: Times Square → Radio City Music Hall → Rockefeller Plaza → Channel Gardens → St. Patrick's Cathedral.

What is the best observation deck near Times Square?

For most first-time visitors, Top of the Rock is the best choice. The biggest advantage is the view of the Empire State Building and Central Park. Top of the Rock offers the best classic NYC skyline view and best first-time experience. SUMMIT One Vanderbilt offers the best modern experience and best photography options. You usually do not need to visit multiple observation decks unless skyline views are a major priority. For a full comparison, read the NYC Observation Decks Compared guide.

What can I do near Times Square when it rains?

The best rainy-day attractions near Times Square are the New York Public Library, Grand Central Terminal, MoMA, SUMMIT One Vanderbilt, and Morgan Library & Museum. A great rainy-day route: Times Square → NYPL → Grand Central → SUMMIT → MoMA. This keeps you mostly indoors while still experiencing some of Midtown's best attractions. For a complete bad-weather plan, read: Best Rainy-Day Attractions in NYC

Where should I eat near Times Square?

The best food is usually not inside Times Square. Instead, walk a few minutes to Hell's Kitchen (best for restaurants, Broadway dinners, and local atmosphere) or Koreatown (best for Korean BBQ, late-night meals, and desserts). Times Square restaurants are convenient, but many visitors find them overpriced compared with nearby neighborhoods.

What should I skip near Times Square?

If your time is limited, skip random Times Square souvenir shops, chain restaurants directly on Times Square, spending hours photographing the same area, and multiple observation decks. Instead, spend that time exploring nearby landmarks and neighborhoods.

Is Midtown Manhattan safe to walk around?

Midtown is one of the most visited areas in New York and is generally busy throughout the day and evening. Normal city precautions apply: keep belongings secure in crowded areas, be aware around busy tourist zones, and avoid blocking sidewalks. The busiest areas, including Times Square, Rockefeller Center, and Fifth Avenue, usually have constant foot traffic.
